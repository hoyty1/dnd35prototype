## 1. Introduction

### What is Mounted Combat?
Mounted combat is a dynamic fighting style where you ride a creature (typically a horse) into battle. Your mount becomes an extension of your combat capabilities, providing mobility, tactical advantages, and devastating charge attacks.

### Why Use Mounts?
**Speed & Mobility**
- Move faster than on foot (horses typically have 40-60 ft. speed)
- Cover ground quickly in outdoor encounters
- Chase down fleeing enemies or escape dangerous situations

**Combat Advantages**
- +1 AC bonus against opponents on foot
- Powerful mounted charges (double or triple damage with feats)
- Height advantage for better positioning
- Your mount can attack independently (if war-trained)

**Tactical Flexibility**
- Hit-and-run tactics with Ride-By Attack
- Mounted archery for mobile ranged combat
- Trample through enemy lines
- Control the battlefield with superior positioning

### Overview of Benefits
✅ **Increased Movement**: Travel 2-3x faster than on foot  
✅ **Charge Specialist**: Devastating lance charges with Spirited Charge feat  
✅ **Defensive Bonus**: +1 AC vs. foot soldiers, +4 AC with Cover  
✅ **Action Economy**: Your mount acts on your turn (controlled mounts)  
✅ **Versatility**: Works for melee fighters, archers, and hybrid builds

## 2. Getting Started

### How to Acquire a Mount
**Purchase from Vendors**
- Visit stables, ranchers, or animal merchants in towns
- Prices range from 30 gp (pony) to 400 gp (heavy warhorse)
- War-trained mounts cost 50-150 gp more than regular versions

**Other Methods**
- Paladin's special mount (class feature at 5th level)
- Druid's animal companion
- Purchased as starting equipment
- Captured/tamed in the wild (Handle Animal checks)

### Mount Types Available
The game features **9 different mount types**:

| Category | Mounts |
|----------|--------|
| **Light Mounts** | Pony, Light Horse, Light Warhorse |
| **Medium Mounts** | War Pony, Warpony (heavy) |
| **Heavy Mounts** | Heavy Horse, Heavy Warhorse |
| **Exotic** | Riding Dog, Dire Wolf* |

*Some exotic mounts may require special training or class features

### Choosing the Right Mount for Your Character

#### By Character Size
- **Small characters (Halflings, Gnomes)**: Pony, War Pony, Riding Dog
- **Medium characters (Humans, Elves, Dwarves)**: Any horse (Light, Heavy, Warhorse)
- **Size matters!** You must be at least one size smaller than your mount

#### By Combat Role
**🗡️ Melee Fighters (Paladins, Fighters, Knights)**
- **Best Choice**: Heavy Warhorse
- **Why**: High HP, good AC, war-trained, powerful attacks
- **Speed**: 50 ft. (good balance)
- **Perfect for**: Lance charges, frontline combat

**🏹 Mounted Archers (Rangers, Scouts)**
- **Best Choice**: Light Horse or Light Warhorse
- **Why**: High speed (60 ft.), good maneuverability
- **Speed**: 60 ft. (excellent mobility)
- **Perfect for**: Hit-and-run tactics, kiting enemies

**⚔️ Versatile Combatants (Multi-class, Hybrid)**
- **Best Choice**: Light Warhorse
- **Why**: Balance of speed (60 ft.) and combat ability
- **War-trained**: Can fight when controlled
- **Perfect for**: Flexible tactics, adapting to situations

**🐕 Small Character Specialists**
- **Best Choice**: War Pony
- **Why**: Proportional to rider size, war-trained
- **Speed**: 40 ft. (adequate)
- **Perfect for**: Halfling/Gnome fighters and paladins

## 3. Basic Mounting & Dismounting

### How to Mount (Step-by-Step)

**Standard Mounting (Move Action)**
1. **Position yourself** adjacent to your mount
2. **Select "Mount" action** from your action menu
3. **No check required** for standard mounting
4. **Cost**: 1 move action
5. **Result**: You are now mounted and occupy your mount's space

**Requirements:**
- Mount must be at least one size larger than you
- Mount must be willing or trained
- You must have at least one hand free
- Mount cannot be prone

### How to Dismount

**Standard Dismounting (Move Action)**
1. **Select "Dismount" action** from your action menu
2. **No check required** for standard dismounting
3. **Cost**: 1 move action
4. **Choose landing square** (any adjacent square to mount)
5. **Result**: You are now on foot

**Landing:**
- You can dismount to any square adjacent to your mount
- You land on your feet (no falling damage)
- You can move afterward if you have movement remaining

### Fast Mounting/Dismounting (DC 20 Ride Check)

**Fast Mount (Free Action)**
- **Ride Check**: DC 20
- **Cost**: Free action (if successful)
- **Benefit**: Mount as part of your regular move
- **On Failure**: You fall prone next to the mount

**Example:**
> **Situation**: Tordek the dwarf fighter needs to mount quickly to pursue fleeing orcs.
> 
> **Action**: Tordek attempts a fast mount (DC 20 Ride check)
> - Ride bonus: +8 (5 ranks, +3 Dex)
> - Roll: 14 + 8 = 22 ✅ **Success!**
> - Result: Tordek mounts as a free action and still has his full move and standard action

**Fast Dismount (Free Action)**
- **Ride Check**: DC 20
- **Cost**: Free action (if successful)
- **Benefit**: Dismount and continue moving
- **On Failure**: You fall prone in a random adjacent square

**Example:**
> **Situation**: Lidda the halfling rogue needs to dismount and tumble past an enemy.
> 
> **Action**: Lidda attempts a fast dismount (DC 20 Ride check)
> - Ride bonus: +10 (8 ranks, +2 Dex)
> - Roll: 12 + 10 = 22 ✅ **Success!**
> - Result: Lidda dismounts as a free action, then uses her move action to Tumble

### Action Economy

| Mounting Method | Action Type | Ride Check | Notes |
|-----------------|-------------|------------|-------|
| **Standard Mount** | Move Action | None | Always succeeds |
| **Fast Mount** | Free Action | DC 20 | Falls prone on failure |
| **Standard Dismount** | Move Action | None | Always succeeds |
| **Fast Dismount** | Free Action | DC 20 | Falls prone on failure |

**Action Economy Example:**
```
Standard Turn (on foot):
- Move action: Walk to horse
- Move action: Mount horse
= No actions left for combat

Fast Mount Turn:
- Free action: Fast mount (DC 20 check)
- Move action: Ride toward enemy
- Standard action: Attack!
```

### Common Mistakes

❌ **Trying to mount while in melee threat range without Mobility**
- Mounting provokes attacks of opportunity
- Dismounting also provokes attacks of opportunity
- Solution: 5-foot step away first, or take the AoO

❌ **Forgetting you need a move action to mount**
- Many new players think mounting is a free action by default
- You must succeed at DC 20 Ride check for free action mounting
- Budget your actions accordingly

❌ **Mounting a creature your size**
- You must be at least one size smaller than your mount
- Medium characters can't ride Medium mounts
- Small characters can't ride Small mounts

❌ **Attempting to fast mount without Ride skill**
- DC 20 is challenging for untrained characters
- Untrained Ride check = 1d20 + Dex modifier
- Invest at least 3-5 ranks in Ride for consistency

❌ **Forgetting your mount's position after dismounting**
- Your mount doesn't disappear—it stays on the battlefield
- Position matters for tactical control
- Remember to command your mount on subsequent turns

> ⚠️ **Important**: Mounting and dismounting both provoke attacks of opportunity from threatening enemies!

## 4. The Ride Skill

### Importance of Ride Skill
The **Ride skill** (PHB p.80) is absolutely essential for mounted combat. Without it, you'll struggle with basic tasks and won't be able to perform advanced maneuvers. Every mounted combatant should invest heavily in this skill.

**Key Benefits:**
- Control your mount in combat situations
- Perform advanced maneuvers (charges, leaps, cover)
- Stay in the saddle when attacked
- Fast mount/dismount for tactical flexibility
- Reduce ranged attack penalties (with Mounted Archery feat)

**Recommended Investment:**
- **Minimum**: 5 ranks (for consistency with DC 5-10 tasks)
- **Competent**: 8-10 ranks (for DC 15 tasks with good reliability)
- **Master**: 15+ ranks (for DC 20 tasks and extreme situations)

### All Ride Skill DCs with Examples

#### DC 5: Basic Control Tasks

**Guide with Knees**
- **Task**: Control mount with your knees while using both hands for other purposes
- **Use**: Wielding two-handed weapons, using a bow, casting spells with somatic components
- **Check**: Made each round you need both hands free
- **Failure**: Mount not controlled, or you must use one hand to guide

**Example:**
> **Fighter wielding greatsword on horseback**
> - Round 1: Makes DC 5 Ride check to guide with knees → Success → Attacks with greatsword (two hands)
> - Round 2: Fails DC 5 check → Must choose: use one hand for reins (1d10 one-handed) or lose control

**Stay in Saddle (Reroll)**
- **Task**: React instinctively to avoid falling when mount acts unexpectedly
- **Use**: Automatic check when mount rears, jumps, or makes sudden movements
- **Check**: Made reactively when triggered
- **Failure**: Fall from mount (see Soft Fall below)

**Example:**
> **Wizard's horse startled by fireball**
> - Horse rears unexpectedly
> - Wizard makes DC 5 Ride check
> - Roll: 3 + 2 (Dex) = 5 ✅ **Success!**
> - Stays in saddle, can act normally

---

#### DC 10: Combat Readiness

**Fight with Warhorse**
- **Task**: Control a war-trained mount in combat
- **Use**: Directing your mount to attack independently
- **Check**: Made each round you want mount to attack
- **Failure**: Mount doesn't attack (becomes uncontrolled that round)

**Example:**
> **Paladin on heavy warhorse charging orcs**
> - Paladin attacks orc with lance
> - Wants warhorse to bite and hoof the same orc
> - Makes DC 10 Ride check → Success → Warhorse gets full attack
> - Total: Lance attack + Bite + 2 hoof attacks!

**Cover (Reaction)**
- **Task**: Drop down and hang alongside mount for cover
- **Use**: Reaction to incoming attacks, especially ranged
- **Check**: Made as immediate action when attacked
- **Benefit**: +4 cover bonus to AC
- **Limitation**: Can't attack while using cover

**Example:**
> **Ranger notices enemy archers taking aim**
> - Enemy archer attacks: 1d20+8 vs AC 16
> - Ranger uses Cover as immediate action (DC 10 Ride check)
> - Roll: 12 + 6 = 18 ✅ **Success!**
> - AC increases to 20 (16 + 4 cover)
> - Arrow misses!

---

#### DC 15: Advanced Maneuvers

**Soft Fall**
- **Task**: Take no damage when falling from mount
- **Use**: Automatic when knocked off or mount killed
- **Check**: Made when falling/thrown from mount
- **Success**: Land on feet, no damage
- **Failure**: Fall prone, take falling damage if applicable

**Example:**
> **Fighter knocked off horse by giant's club**
> - Giant hits fighter: "You're knocked off your mount!"
> - Fighter makes DC 15 Ride check
> - Roll: 13 + 5 = 18 ✅ **Success!**
> - Fighter lands gracefully on feet, ready to fight

**Leap**
- **Task**: Cause mount to jump obstacles
- **Use**: Clearing barriers, chasms, or difficult terrain
- **Check**: Made when attempting jump
- **Success**: Mount jumps obstacle (if physically possible)
- **Failure**: Mount refuses or stops short

**Example:**
> **Knight needs to jump over fallen log during chase**
> - Log is 4 feet high, horse can physically clear it
> - Makes DC 15 Ride check
> - Roll: 9 + 7 = 16 ✅ **Success!**
> - Horse leaps log, chase continues!

**Spur Mount**
- **Task**: Encourage mount to move faster temporarily
- **Use**: When you need extra speed for one round
- **Benefit**: +10 feet to mount's speed for 1 round
- **Cost**: 1 point of damage to mount
- **Limitation**: Can't spur exhausted mount

**Example:**
> **Paladin charging toward endangered ally**
> - Heavy warhorse normal speed: 50 ft.
> - Uses Spur Mount (DC 15 Ride check)
> - Roll: 11 + 8 = 19 ✅ **Success!**
> - Speed increases to 60 ft. for this round
> - Mount takes 1 damage
> - Reaches ally in time to intercept attacker!

---

#### DC 20: Expert Control

**Control Mount in Battle**
- **Task**: Control a mount not trained for combat (non-warhorse)
- **Use**: Using regular horses/ponies in combat situations
- **Check**: Made each round to maintain control
- **Failure**: Mount panics and flees or refuses commands

**Example:**
> **Ranger on regular light horse engaged in melee**
> - Light horse not war-trained (panics near combat)
> - Each round: DC 20 Ride check to maintain control
> - Round 1: Roll 15 + 10 = 25 ✅ Horse controlled
> - Round 2: Roll 8 + 10 = 18 ❌ Horse panics and flees!

> ⚠️ **This is why war-trained mounts are essential for combat!**

**Fast Mount/Dismount**
- **Task**: Mount or dismount as a free action
- **Use**: Tactical situations requiring swift mounting/dismounting
- **Check**: Made when attempting fast mount/dismount
- **Success**: Mount/dismount as free action
- **Failure**: Fall prone adjacent to mount

**Example:**
> **Rogue needs to mount mid-combat**
> - Standard mount = move action (no attack this turn)
> - Fast mount = free action (can still attack)
> - Makes DC 20 Ride check
> - Roll: 16 + 9 = 25 ✅ **Success!**
> - Mounts as free action
> - Still has move and standard action available!

### How to Improve Ride Skill

**Skill Ranks**
- **+1 per rank** (maximum ranks = character level + 3)
- **Class skill bonus**: +3 if Ride is a class skill and you have 1+ rank
- **Best classes**: Fighter, Paladin, Ranger (all class skills)

**Ability Modifier**
- Ride is **Dexterity-based**
- Each +1 Dex modifier = +1 to Ride checks
- Consider taking Dexterity 14+ for mounted characters

**Gear & Equipment**
- **Military Saddle**: +2 circumstance bonus to Ride checks to stay in saddle
- **Masterwork Saddle**: +4 circumstance bonus (if available)
- **Spurs**: Useful for Spur Mount attempts (not a bonus, just thematic)

**Feats**
- **Skill Focus (Ride)**: +3 bonus to all Ride checks
- **Mounted Combat**: Doesn't improve checks, but negates hits on mount

**Magic Items**
- **Horseshoes of Speed**: Doesn't affect Ride checks, but improves mount speed
- **Saddle of Cavalry**: May provide bonuses (DM discretion)
- **Gloves of Dexterity**: Improve Dex modifier → improve Ride checks

**Example Progression:**
```
Level 1 Fighter (Mounted Combatant Build)
- Base ranks: 4 ranks
- Dexterity: 14 (+2 modifier)
- Class skill bonus: +3
- Military saddle: +2 (for stay in saddle)
- Total: +9 to Ride (+11 for staying in saddle)

Level 5 Fighter (Specialized)
- Base ranks: 8 ranks
- Dexterity: 16 (+3 modifier, from level 4 ability increase)
- Class skill bonus: +3
- Military saddle: +2 (stay in saddle)
- Skill Focus (Ride): +3
- Total: +17 to Ride (+19 for staying in saddle)
```

**🛡️ Paladin**
- **Ride is class skill**: Natural synergy

**⚔️ Fighter**

- **Best for**: Charge specialists, lance masters

**🏹 Ranger**
- **Ride is class skill**: Natural outdoorsman
- **Track**: Find enemies to chase down
- **Archery combat style**: Perfect for mounted archery
- **Best for**: Mounted archers, skirmishers

- **Animal companion**: Can be mount (if appropriate size)

- **Ride is NOT class skill**: Very challenging
- **No armor**: Vulnerable while mounted

- **Best for**: Generally avoid mounted combat

## 5. Controlling Your Mount

### Controlled vs Uncontrolled States

**Controlled Mount**
- You actively direct your mount's actions
- Mount acts on your initiative count
- You can command mount to move and attack (if war-trained)
- Requires successful Ride checks for certain actions
- **Default state** for skilled riders on war-trained mounts

**Uncontrolled Mount**
- Mount acts independently on its own initiative
- Mount makes its own decisions (usually self-preservation)
- You're just a passenger—mount moves where it wants
- Can only use move action to guide mount where you want to go
- **Default state** for untrained riders or panicked mounts

> 🔑 **Key Distinction**: Controlled = you command, Uncontrolled = mount decides

---

### War-Trained vs Regular Mounts

**War-Trained Mounts** (Warhorses, War Ponies)
- **Cost**: 50-150 gp more than regular version
- **Combat Training**: Won't panic in battle
- **Can Attack**: Will fight enemies when controlled
- **Easier Control**: Only DC 10 Ride check to fight with warhorse
- **Examples**: Light Warhorse, Heavy Warhorse, War Pony

**Regular Mounts** (Horses, Ponies, Riding Dogs)
- **Cost**: Cheaper (30-200 gp)
- **No Combat Training**: Panic easily near violence
- **Won't Attack**: Flee or freeze when threatened
- **Hard to Control**: DC 20 Ride check each round in combat
- **Examples**: Light Horse, Heavy Horse, Pony

**Comparison:**
```
Scenario: Charging into melee combat

War-Trained Mount:
- DC 10 Ride check to control → Usually succeeds
- Mount attacks enemies with bite/hooves
- Stays in combat reliably
✅ Recommended for combat

Regular Mount:
- DC 20 Ride check each round → Often fails
- Mount panics and flees when check fails
- No combat contribution
❌ Not suitable for combat
```

> ⚠️ **Critical**: Always buy war-trained mounts for combat characters! The extra 75-150 gp is absolutely worth it.

---

### What Your Mount Can Do When Controlled

**Movement**
- Move up to its speed on your turn
- You decide direction and destination
- Can charge (mount + rider both charge)
- Can withdraw, run, or hustle

**Standard Actions** (War-Trained Only)
- **Full Attack**: Bite and 2 hoof attacks (for horses)
- **Single Attack**: One natural weapon attack
- **Ready Action**: Hold action for specific trigger
- **Cannot**: Use items, cast spells, or perform complex tasks

**Free Actions**
- Can communicate distress (whinny, snort)
- Can fall prone voluntarily

**Your Actions While Mounted**
- You can attack independently
- You can cast spells (with DC 5 Ride check for somatic components)
- You can use items
- You can make Ride checks for special maneuvers

**Example Turn (Controlled War-Trained Mount):**
```
Paladin's Turn (Initiative 18):
1. Move: Mount moves 50 ft. toward enemy
2. Standard: Paladin attacks with longsword
3. Mount's Attack: DC 10 Ride check → Success → 
   Mount makes bite and 2 hoof attacks!

Result: 4 total attacks (1 longsword, 1 bite, 2 hooves)
```

---

### What Happens When You Lose Control

**Immediate Effect**
- Mount becomes uncontrolled for that round (or longer)
- Mount acts on its own initiative (roll separately)
- You lose ability to direct mount's actions

**Mount Behavior (Uncontrolled)**

**Regular Mounts:**
- **In Combat**: Flee from danger (usually away from enemies)
- **Direction**: Away from nearest threat
- **Speed**: Run action (4x speed) in panic
- **Attacks**: None (self-preservation only)

**War-Trained Mounts:**
- **In Combat**: May stand ground or defend self
- **Direction**: Stays near rider, but chooses own position
- **Speed**: Normal move or withdraw
- **Attacks**: May attack nearest threat if cornered

**Regaining Control**
- **Next Round**: Make appropriate Ride check again
  - DC 10 for war-trained mounts
  - DC 20 for regular mounts in combat
- **Success**: Regain control, mount acts on your initiative again
- **Failure**: Mount remains uncontrolled for another round

**Example:**
> **Fighter on regular light horse in melee combat**
> 
> **Round 1**: Fighter makes DC 20 Ride check → Roll 12 + 6 = 18 ❌ **Fails!**
> - Horse panics and flees 240 ft. (60 ft. speed x4 run)
> - Fighter is carried away from combat
> 
> **Round 2**: Fighter makes DC 20 Ride check again → Roll 17 + 6 = 23 ✅ **Success!**
> - Fighter regains control 240 ft. from battle
> - Must spend several rounds riding back to fight

---

### Mount Panic and Fleeing

**Triggers for Panic** (Regular Mounts Only)

**Combat Situations:**
- Entering melee range of enemy creatures
- Being damaged by any attack
- Loud noises (explosions, thunderclaps)
- Fire or extreme environmental effects
- Smelling blood or seeing death

**Panic Effects:**
- Mount becomes uncontrolled (if controlled)
- Mount flees away from threat source
- Mount uses run action (4x speed)
- You must make DC 5 Ride check to stay in saddle while fleeing
- Mount won't stop until it feels safe (200+ ft. usually)

**Preventing Panic:**
- **Use War-Trained Mounts**: Won't panic from combat
- **High Ride Skill**: DC 20 check each round prevents panic behavior
- **Calm Animals Spell**: Can calm panicked mount (Handle Animal check)
- **Distance**: Keep regular mounts far from frontline combat

**War-Trained Mounts DON'T Panic:**
- Specifically trained to ignore combat stress
- Will stay in melee without fleeing
- Won't spook from fire, noise, or blood
- Much more reliable for any combat situation

**Example:**
> **Wizard on regular pony vs bugbears**
> 
> **Setup**: Wizard rides pony (not war-trained) into ambush
> 
> **Bugbear attacks pony:**
> - Pony takes 7 damage
> - Pony panics! (regular mount, took damage)
> - Wizard makes DC 20 Ride check to control: Roll 5 + 2 = 7 ❌ **Fails!**
> 
> **Result:**
> - Pony flees at 160 ft. (40 ft. x4 run)
> - Wizard makes DC 5 Ride check to stay in saddle: Roll 11 + 2 = 13 ✅ **Success**
> - Wizard stays mounted but is carried far from battle
> - Wizard's turn wasted, party down one spellcaster

> 💡 **Lesson**: This is why war-trained mounts are ESSENTIAL for any combat scenario!

## 6. Combat on Horseback

### Initiative (You and Mount Act Together)

**Unified Initiative**
- When mounted (controlled), you and your mount share the same initiative count
- Roll initiative once, both act on that count
- Mount's actions are part of your turn
- This applies ONLY to controlled mounts

**Turn Order:**
```
Example Combat:
- Fighter (mounted, controlled): 18
- Orc Warrior: 15
- Wizard: 12
- Fighter's Horse: (acts on 18 with fighter)

Fighter's Turn (Initiative 18):
1. Mount moves 50 ft.
2. Fighter attacks with lance
3. Mount attacks with bite/hooves (if DC 10 Ride check succeeds)
```

**Uncontrolled Mounts:**
- Roll separate initiative for uncontrolled mounts
- Mount acts on its own count
- You act on your count (as a passenger)

**Tactical Advantage:**
- Higher initiative = mount moves before enemies
- You can position mount and attack in same turn
- Enables devastating alpha strikes

---

### Attacking While Mounted

**Melee Attacks**
- You can make melee attacks normally while mounted
- Use any melee weapon (one-handed or two-handed)
- Reach weapons work normally
- You threaten all squares around your mount

**Two-Handed Weapons:**
- Require DC 5 Ride check each round (guide with knees)
- **Success**: Attack normally with both hands
- **Failure**: Must use one hand for reins (can't use two-handed weapon effectively)

**Example:**
> **Fighter with Greatsword on Heavy Warhorse**
> - Each round: DC 5 Ride check to guide with knees
> - Roll: 7 + 5 = 12 ✅ **Success**
> - Fighter attacks with greatsword (2d6+9 damage)
> - If failed: Must drop to one hand (1d10+6 damage) or stop attacking

**Reach and Threatened Squares:**
- You threaten squares based on your weapon's reach
- Your mount's space counts as your space
- Large mount = you control more battlefield area

**Mount's Attacks:**
- War-trained mounts can attack when controlled (DC 10 Ride check)
- Mounts get full attack routine: bite + 2 hooves (for horses)
- Your attack and mount's attacks are separate
- **Example**: Fighter attacks once with lance, horse attacks three times (bite + 2 hooves)

---

### AC Bonuses (+1 vs Opponents on Foot)

**Height Advantage:**
- +1 AC bonus against melee attacks from foot soldiers
- Applies to opponents smaller than your mount
- Represents elevated position and harder-to-reach angles
- **Stacks** with other AC bonuses

**Example:**
> **Paladin on Heavy Warhorse (AC 18 base)**
> - vs. Orc on foot: AC 19 (18 + 1 height advantage)
> - vs. Another mounted opponent: AC 18 (no bonus)
> - vs. Large Giant: AC 18 (giant is taller, no bonus)

**Cover Bonus:**
- Additional +4 AC if you use Cover maneuver (DC 10 Ride check, immediate action)
- Can't attack while using Cover
- Great for avoiding ranged attacks

**Combined Example:**
> **Ranger on Light Warhorse**
> - Base AC: 17 (armor + Dex)
> - vs. Goblin archer (ground): 18 (17 + 1 height)
> - Goblin shoots: Ranger uses Cover (DC 10 Ride check)
> - Roll: 14 + 8 = 22 ✅ **Success**
> - AC becomes: 22 (17 + 1 height + 4 cover)
> - Arrow misses!

### Ranged Combat Penalties

**Standard Ranged Attack Penalty:**
- **-4 penalty** to ranged attack rolls while mounted and moving
- Applies to: bows, crossbows, thrown weapons, ranged spells with attack rolls
- Reason: Mount's movement makes aiming difficult
- **Does NOT apply** if mount doesn't move that round

**Mounted Archery Feat:**
- Reduces penalty from -4 to -2
- **Essential** for mounted archer builds
- Still -2 penalty, but much more manageable

**Comparison:**
```
Ranger on Moving Horse (no Mounted Archery):
- Base attack bonus: +6
- Dexterity bonus: +4
- Composite longbow: +1
- Mounted moving penalty: -4
= Total: +7 to hit

Ranger on Moving Horse (with Mounted Archery):
- Base attack bonus: +6
- Dexterity bonus: +4
- Composite longbow: +1
- Mounted moving penalty: -2 (reduced!)
= Total: +9 to hit
```

**Standing Still:**
- If your mount doesn't move that round, NO penalty
- You can ready action: "Hold until after mount moves, then shoot with no penalty"
- Tactical consideration: sacrifice mobility for accuracy

**Example:**
> **Mounted Archer vs. Orc (AC 15)**
> 
> **Option 1**: Move and Shoot
> - Horse moves 60 ft.
> - Ranger shoots (+7 attack with -4 penalty)
> - Roll: 10 + 7 = 17 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!**
> - Advantage: Maintains mobility
> 
> **Option 2**: Stand and Shoot
> - Horse doesn't move
> - Ranger shoots (+11 attack, no penalty)
> - Roll: 7 + 11 = 18 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!**
> - Advantage: Better accuracy, but static position

### Using Two Hands While Mounted (Guide with Knees DC 5)

**When Required:**
- **Two-handed melee weapons** (greatsword, greataxe, lance)
- **Bows** (longbow, shortbow)
- **Two-weapon fighting** (sword and dagger)
- **Spells with somatic components** (most spells)
- **Using items** (drinking potions, wielding shields and weapons)

**How It Works:**
- Make DC 5 Ride check each round you need both hands
- **Success**: Control mount with your knees/legs, hands free
- **Failure**: Must use one hand for reins (can't perform two-handed actions effectively)

**Frequency:**
- Must check EACH ROUND you're using both hands
- Even skilled riders can fail occasionally
- Invest in high Ride bonus for consistency

**Examples:**

**Fighter with Greatsword:**
> **Round 1**: DC 5 Ride check → 12 ✅ Success → Attacks with greatsword (2d6+9)
> **Round 2**: DC 5 Ride check → 3 ❌ Failure → Must use reins, greatsword ineffective
> **Round 3**: DC 5 Ride check → 9 ✅ Success → Back to greatsword attacks

**Wizard Casting Fireball:**
> - Fireball requires somatic components (both hands)
> - Makes DC 5 Ride check → 15 ✅ Success
> - Casts fireball normally while mounted
> - If failed: Can't complete somatic components, spell fails

**Archer Shooting Longbow:**
> - Every round shooting requires DC 5 Ride check
> - Success: Draw and shoot normally
> - Failure: Can't nock arrow effectively (no attack that round)

**Consistency:**
```
Ride Bonus +0: 75% success rate (need 5+ on d20)
Ride Bonus +3: 90% success rate (need 2+ on d20)
Ride Bonus +5: 95% success rate (automatic except natural 1)
Ride Bonus +10: Effectively automatic (can't fail except on nat 1)
```

> 💡 **Tip**: A military saddle provides +2 bonus to "stay in saddle" checks but NOT to "guide with knees" checks. Invest in Ride skill!

### Combat Round Example

**Paladin (Level 5) on Heavy Warhorse vs. Orc Champion**

**Setup:**
- Paladin: AC 19, +7 to hit, 1d8+4 longsword damage
- Heavy Warhorse: AC 14, +6 to hit, bite 1d4+4, hooves 1d6+2 each
- Paladin's Ride: +8
- Orc Champion: AC 15, +8 to hit, 1d12+6 greataxe damage

**Initiative:** Paladin 16, Orc 11

**Round 1 - Paladin's Turn:**

1. **Mount Movement**: Heavy Warhorse moves 50 ft. toward Orc
2. **Paladin Attack**: Attacks Orc with longsword
   - Roll: 15 + 7 = 22 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!**
   - Damage: 1d8+4 = 7 damage
3. **Mount Attack Attempt**: DC 10 Ride check to command warhorse
   - Roll: 12 + 8 = 20 ✅ **Success!**
   - Warhorse attacks with bite and hooves:
     - Bite: Roll 14 + 6 = 20 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!** → 1d4+4 = 6 damage
     - Hoof 1: Roll 8 + 6 = 14 vs AC 15 ❌ **Miss!**
     - Hoof 2: Roll 19 + 6 = 25 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!** → 1d6+2 = 5 damage
4. **Total Damage to Orc**: 7 + 6 + 5 = 18 damage

**Round 1 - Orc's Turn:**

1. **Orc Action**: 5-foot step toward Paladin, attacks with greataxe
   - Roll: 12 + 8 = 20 vs AC 19 (Paladin's AC) ❌ **Miss!**
   - Paladin's +1 AC bonus (height advantage) made the difference!

**Round 2 - Paladin's Turn:**

1. **Mount Movement**: Heavy Warhorse stays in position
2. **Paladin Full Attack**: Attacks Orc twice (BAB +5/+0)
   - Attack 1: Roll 18 + 7 = 25 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!** → 1d8+4 = 8 damage
   - Attack 2: Roll 7 + 2 = 9 vs AC 15 ❌ **Miss!**
3. **Mount Attack**: DC 10 Ride check
   - Roll: 6 + 8 = 14 ✅ **Success!**
   - Bite: Roll 11 + 6 = 17 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!** → 1d4+4 = 5 damage
   - Hoof 1: Roll 15 + 6 = 21 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!** → 1d6+2 = 7 damage
   - Hoof 2: Roll 4 + 6 = 10 vs AC 15 ❌ **Miss!**
4. **Total Damage**: 8 + 5 + 7 = 20 damage
5. **Orc Total Damage**: 38 damage → **Orc drops to 0 HP, combat ends!**

**Analysis:**
- Paladin dealt 45 total damage over 2 rounds (27 from mount, 18 from paladin)
- Mount contributed 60% of total damage output
- +1 AC bonus saved Paladin from taking 13 damage (orc's missed hit)
- Controlled mount = 4 extra attacks (bite + hooves each round)

> ⚡ **This is the power of mounted combat!**

## 7. Mounted Charges

### How to Perform a Mounted Charge

**Requirements:**
1. **Line of sight** to target
2. **Straight or nearly straight path** (no sharp turns)
3. **Clear path** (no obstacles or allies blocking)
4. **Minimum 10 ft. movement** required
5. **Maximum movement** = double your mount's speed
6. **Target must be within charge range**

**Steps:**
1. **Declare charge action** (full-round action)
2. **Select target** within charge range
3. **Mount moves** up to double speed in straight line toward target
4. **Make single melee attack** with +2 bonus
5. **Suffer -2 AC penalty** until your next turn

**Basic Charge Example:**
> **Fighter on Heavy Warhorse (speed 50 ft.)**
> 
> **Situation**: Orc is 80 ft. away, clear path
> 
> **Action**: Declare charge
> - Mount moves 100 ft. (50 x2 double speed)
> - Easily reaches orc at 80 ft.
> - Fighter attacks with longsword: +9 to hit (+7 base +2 charge)
> - Roll: 12 + 9 = 21 vs AC 15 ✅ **Hit!**
> - Damage: 1d8+4 = 9 damage
> - **Downside**: Fighter's AC drops to 17 (19 - 2) until next turn

---

### Charge Bonuses (+2 Attack)

**Standard Charge Bonuses:**
- **+2 bonus** to melee attack roll
- **No** damage bonus (unless using lance or special feats)
- **-2 penalty** to AC until next turn
- **Single attack only** (no full attack)

**When Worth It:**
- Need extra accuracy against high AC target
- Opening move of combat (alpha strike)
- Setting up for special feat effects (Spirited Charge, Ride-By Attack)
- Closing distance quickly to reach enemy

**When NOT Worth It:**
- Target is close (you could full attack instead)
- You'll be surrounded by enemies (AC penalty dangerous)
- Terrain is difficult or blocked
- You'd miss out on better tactical positioning

---

### Movement Requirements

**Straight Line Rule:**
- Must charge in straight line or nearly straight line
- **Allowed**: Minor deviations (5-10 degree angle changes)
- **Not Allowed**: Sharp turns, zigzagging, or circuitous routes

**Terrain Considerations:**
- **Clear terrain**: Can charge full double speed
- **Difficult terrain**: Can't charge through difficult terrain
- **Allies**: Can't charge through squares occupied by allies
- **Obstacles**: Walls, furniture, trees block charges

**Distance:**
- **Minimum**: Must move at least 10 ft.
- **Maximum**: Up to double mount's speed
- **Example**: Heavy Warhorse (50 ft. speed) can charge up to 100 ft.

**Examples:**

**Valid Charge:**
```
Start Position [Fighter on Horse] ... 60 ft. straight line ... [Orc]
✅ Clear path, straight line, within 100 ft. range → Charge succeeds
```

**Invalid Charge:**
```
[Fighter on Horse]
        |
        | 30 ft.
        |
        ----60 ft.---- [Orc]

❌ Requires 90-degree turn → Can't charge
```

**Blocked Charge:**
```
[Fighter on Horse] ... 20 ft. ... [Ally] ... 40 ft. ... [Orc]
❌ Ally blocks path → Can't charge through ally
```

### Spirited Charge Damage (2x or 3x)

**Spirited Charge Feat** (PHB p.100)
- **Prerequisites**: Mounted Combat, Ride 1 rank, Ride-By Attack
- **Benefit**: Deal double damage on mounted charge
- **With Lance**: Deal triple damage on mounted charge

**How It Works:**

**Standard Weapon Charge (Double Damage):**
1. Make successful mounted charge
2. Roll damage normally
3. **Multiply total damage by 2**
4. Applies to ALL damage (weapon + Strength + magic + other bonuses)

**Example:**
> **Fighter charges with longsword**
> - Base damage: 1d8+4 (Strength)
> - Roll: 7 + 4 = 11 damage
> - **Spirited Charge**: 11 x 2 = **22 damage**

**Lance Charge (Triple Damage):**
1. Make successful mounted charge with lance
2. Roll damage normally
3. **Multiply total damage by 3**
4. Already accounts for lance's 2x charge damage, then Spirited Charge doubles that

**Example:**
> **Paladin charges with lance**
> - Base damage: 1d8+4 (Strength)
> - Roll: 6 + 4 = 10 damage
> - **Spirited Charge with lance**: 10 x 3 = **30 damage**
> - **With Smite Evil** (+5 levels): 10 + 5 = 15 → 15 x 3 = **45 damage**

**Damage Comparison:**

| Attack Type | Base Damage | Charge Damage | Spirited Charge |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Longsword (regular) | 1d8+4 | 1d8+4 | 2×(1d8+4) |
| Lance (regular) | 1d8+4 | 2×(1d8+4) | 3×(1d8+4) |
| Lance + Smite Evil | 1d8+4+5 | 2×(1d8+4+5) | 3×(1d8+4+5) |

**Maximizing Spirited Charge Damage:**

1. **Use a Lance**: Automatically gets triple damage
2. **Power Attack**: Add bonus damage before multiplying
3. **Smite Evil/Favored Enemy**: Add class bonuses
4. **Magic Weapons**: Enhancement bonuses multiply too
5. **Strength**: Higher Strength = more damage multiplied

**Devastating Example:**
> **Level 10 Paladin, Strength 18, +2 Lance, Smite Evil, Spirited Charge**
> - Base: 1d8 (lance)
> - Strength: +6 (18 Str with two-hands = 1.5x Str bonus)
> - Magic: +2 (enhancement)
> - Smite Evil: +10 (level bonus)
> - Power Attack: +5 (trading -5 attack for +5 damage)
> 
> **Total before multiplier**: 1d8+6+2+10+5 = 1d8+23
> - Roll: 8 + 23 = 31 damage
> - **Spirited Charge**: 31 x 3 = **93 damage!**
> 
> **With critical hit** (x3 crit):
> - 93 x 3 = **279 damage!** (This can kill dragons!)

> ⚡ **This is why Spirited Charge is one of the most powerful feats in D&D 3.5e!**

## 8. Mounted Combat Feats

### Mounted Combat

**Mounted Combat** (PHB p.98)
- **Prerequisites**: Ride 1 rank
- **Benefit**: Once per round, you may attempt a Ride check (DC = attack roll) to negate a hit against your mount

**How It Works:**
1. Enemy attacks your mount and hits
2. **Immediate action**: Declare you're using Mounted Combat feat
3. Make Ride check, DC = enemy's attack roll result
4. **Success**: Hit negated, mount takes no damage
5. **Failure**: Hit connects, mount takes full damage
6. **Limitation**: Can only use once per round (one negated attack max)

**When to Use:**
- **Critical hits**: Negate devastating crits against mount
- **Heavy damage**: Large damage rolls that could kill mount
- **Limited HP**: Mount is already wounded
- **Boss attacks**: High-damage enemy attacks

**When NOT to Use:**
- **Minor damage**: 2-3 damage from weak enemies (save it for big hits)
- **Mount has high HP**: Can afford to take the hit
- **Multiple enemies**: Better to negate the bigger threat
- **Low Ride bonus**: DC too high to reasonably succeed

**Example:**
> **Paladin's Heavy Warhorse (25 HP remaining) vs. Giant**
> 
> **Giant attacks warhorse:**
> - Attack roll: 23 vs AC 14 ✅ **Hit!**
> - Damage roll: 2d8+10 = 18 damage (would drop warhorse to 7 HP)
> 
> **Paladin uses Mounted Combat:**
> - Ride check DC = 23 (giant's attack roll)
> - Paladin's Ride: +12
> - Roll: 15 + 12 = 27 ✅ **Success!**
> - Hit negated, warhorse takes 0 damage
> - Warhorse still at 25 HP
> 
> **Later that round:**
> - Goblin attacks warhorse: Roll 15 vs AC 14 ✅ **Hit!** (4 damage)
> - Paladin already used Mounted Combat this round
> - Cannot negate second attack
> - Warhorse takes 4 damage → 21 HP

**Build Recommendations:**
- **Essential for**: ALL mounted combat builds
- **Priority**: Take this feat FIRST (prerequisite for other mounted feats)
- **Synergy**: High Ride skill makes this feat more effective
- **Investment**: Every +5 to Ride = 25% better chance to negate attacks

**Tactical Usage:**

**Scenario 1: Boss Fight**
> - Dragon attacks mount: 2d8+12 = 24 damage (potentially deadly)
> - Use Mounted Combat: DC 28 Ride check
> - Success: Save mount from massive damage
> - Worth it: Absolutely!

**Scenario 2: Trash Mob**
> - Kobold attacks mount: 1d4+1 = 3 damage
> - Don't use Mounted Combat: Save it for bigger threats
> - Mount can afford minor chip damage

**Maximizing Effectiveness:**
```
Ride Skill Priorities:
- Level 1-4: Invest heavily (max ranks)
- Level 5+: Keep maxed, consider Skill Focus (Ride)
- Level 10+: Ride bonus should be +15 or higher

With +15 Ride:
- DC 20: 75% success (need 5+)
- DC 25: 50% success (need 10+)
- DC 30: 25% success (need 15+)
```

> 🛡️ **Tip**: Announce "I have Mounted Combat" at start of each combat so DM remembers you can negate hits!

### Ride-By Attack

**Ride-By Attack** (PHB p.99)
- **Prerequisites**: Mounted Combat, Ride 1 rank
- **Benefit**: When mounted and charging, you can move and attack, then continue moving afterward

**How It Works:**

**Standard Charge (Without Feat):**
1. Charge enemy
2. Attack enemy
3. **Stop** adjacent to enemy
4. Stuck in melee range until next turn

**Ride-By Attack Charge (With Feat):**
1. Charge toward enemy
2. Attack enemy when you reach them
3. **Continue moving** after attack
4. End movement anywhere within charge range
5. Escape melee range, avoid retaliation

**Tactical Advantages:**
- **Hit-and-run**: Attack and escape before enemies can counter
- **Avoid AoOs**: Don't stay in melee range
- **Reposition**: End in better tactical position
- **Kiting**: Mobile skirmishing tactics
- **Safety**: Minimize damage taken

**Example:**
> **Fighter on Light Warhorse vs. Ogre**
> 
> **Without Ride-By Attack:**
> - Charges ogre 80 ft. away
> - Attacks with lance: Hit for 18 damage
> - **Stops** adjacent to ogre
> - Ogre's turn: Full attack on fighter (2 greatclub attacks)
> 
> **With Ride-By Attack:**
> - Charges ogre 80 ft. away
> - Attacks with lance: Hit for 18 damage
> - **Continues moving** another 20 ft. past ogre
> - Now 20 ft. away from ogre
> - Ogre's turn: Can only move toward fighter (no full attack)

**Movement Breakdown:**
```
Example Charge with Ride-By Attack:
- Mount speed: 60 ft.
- Charge range: 120 ft. (double speed)

Timeline:
Start [Fighter] ....60 ft.... [Ogre] ....60 ft.... End
       ↓                        ↓                    ↓
    Initial              Attack point           Final position
    
Total movement: 120 ft.
Attack occurs: At 60 ft. mark
Continues: Additional 60 ft. after attack
```

**Synergy with Other Feats:**

**Ride-By Attack + Spirited Charge:**
> - Charge enemy
> - Deal 3× lance damage (devastating)
> - Continue moving away
> - Enemy can't retaliate
> - **Perfect combo for "jousting" playstyle**

**Ride-By Attack + Mounted Archery:**
> - Charge into range
> - Attack with lance
> - Continue moving
> - Next round: Shoot with bow from safe distance
> - **Versatile hit-and-run tactics**

**Build Recommendations:**
- **Essential for**: Mobile fighters, skirmishers, archers
- **Less Important for**: Heavy cavalry (paladins who want to stay in melee)
- **Required for**: Spirited Charge feat (prerequisite)
- **Playstyle**: Favors DEX-based or mobile builds

**Advanced Tactics:**

**Scenario 1: Multiple Enemies**
> **Setup**: 3 orcs in a line, 20 ft. apart
> 
> **Action**: Charge with Ride-By Attack
> - Attack Orc 1 (lance: 22 damage)
> - Continue moving
> - Attack Orc 2? (Only get one attack on charge)
> - End movement 30 ft. past last orc
> - **Result**: Hit one, avoided melee with all three

**Scenario 2: Ranged Harassment**
> **Round 1**: Charge archer, attack with lance (30 damage)
> - Continue moving 40 ft. away
> 
> **Round 2**: Archer shoots, moves away
> 
> **Round 3**: Charge again with Ride-By Attack
> - Attack archer again (28 damage, archer dies)
> - Continue moving to safety
> 
> **Result**: Archer never got good attack opportunity

**Scenario 3: Tank Avoidance**
> **Setup**: Enemy fighter (high AC, heavily armored)
> 
> **Strategy**: 
> - Charge past fighter using Ride-By Attack
> - Attack, continue past him
> - Reach vulnerable backline (wizard, cleric)
> - Next round: Threaten casters, not wasting time on tank

**Terrain Considerations:**
- **Open terrain**: Best for Ride-By Attack (long straight lines)
- **Tight quarters**: Less effective (can't continue moving far)
- **Difficult terrain**: Can't charge through it
- **Obstacles**: May block continued movement

> 🏃 **Tip**: Think of Ride-By Attack as "jousting"—hit hard and ride past, don't get stuck in slugfest!

### Trample

**Trample** (PHB p.101)
- **Prerequisites**: Mounted Combat, Ride 1 rank
- **Benefit**: Attempt to overrun opponents while mounted, and your mount may make one hoof attack against any target you knock down

**How It Works:**

**Standard Overrun:**
1. Declare overrun attempt as standard action
2. Target can choose to avoid (step aside) or resist
3. If target resists: Opposed Strength check
4. Winner pushes through, loser knocked prone or blocked

**Trample (With Feat):**
1. Declare overrun while mounted
2. Target **cannot** avoid (must resist)
3. Opposed Strength check: **Use mount's Strength**
4. Success: Target knocked prone, mount deals one hoof attack
5. Failure: Your movement stops

**Why It's Good:**
- Forces enemies prone (easier to hit, -4 AC penalty)
- Extra hoof attack (free damage)
- Uses mount's Strength (usually higher than yours)
- **Can trample multiple enemies** in one charge if you succeed

**Example:**
> **Paladin on Heavy Warhorse (Strength 18, +4) vs. Goblin (Strength 10, +0)**
> 
> **Trample Attempt:**
> - Paladin declares overrun with Trample
> - Goblin must resist (can't avoid)
> - Opposed Strength: Warhorse +4 vs Goblin +0
> - Warhorse rolls: 12 + 4 = 16
> - Goblin rolls: 8 + 0 = 8
> - **Warhorse wins!**
> - Goblin knocked prone (-4 AC, can't attack this turn)
> - Warhorse makes hoof attack: 1d6+4 = 8 damage
> - Paladin continues moving

**Multiple Tramples:**
If you knock down first target, you can continue and attempt to trample another enemy in your path!

**Example:**
> **Fighter charging through 3 goblins in a line**
> 
> **Goblin 1:**
> - Trample: Warhorse Str 18 vs Goblin Str 10
> - Success: Goblin 1 prone, hoof attack (7 damage)
> - Continue moving
> 
> **Goblin 2:**
> - Trample: Success again
> - Goblin 2 prone, hoof attack (6 damage)
> - Continue moving
> 
> **Goblin 3:**
> - Trample: Failure (bad roll)
> - Movement stops
> - Can't reach objective
> 
> **Result**: 2 goblins prone and damaged, but couldn't break through completely

**Tactical Considerations:**

**When to Use Trample:**
✅ **Multiple weak enemies** in a line (easy Strength checks)
✅ **Need to break through** enemy formations
✅ **Enemies have low Strength** (goblins, kobolds, wizards)
✅ **Want to prone targets** for allies to attack with advantage

**When NOT to Use:**
❌ **Single tough enemy** (better to just attack normally)
❌ **High-Strength enemies** (barbarians, fighters, ogres)
❌ **Need to deal max damage** (regular lance charge better)
❌ **Tight quarters** (not enough room to maneuver)

**Strength Check Odds:**

| Your Mount Str | Target Str | Approximate Success Rate |

### Mounted Archery

**Mounted Archery** (PHB p.98)
- **Prerequisites**: Mounted Combat, Ride 1 rank
- **Benefit**: Ranged attack penalty while mounted is halved (from -4 to -2)

**How It Works:**

**Without Mounted Archery:**
- Ranged attacks while mounted and moving: **-4 penalty**
- Makes archery nearly unusable while mobile

**With Mounted Archery:**
- Ranged attacks while mounted and moving: **-2 penalty**
- Much more manageable, viable playstyle

**Standing Still:**
- If mount doesn't move: **No penalty** (with or without feat)
- But defeats purpose of mounted mobility

**Penalty Comparison:**

| Situation | Without Feat | With Feat | Difference |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| **Mount moves** | -4 | -2 | +2 to hit |
| **Mount stationary** | +0 | +0 | No change |

**Example:**
> **Ranger on Light Horse, Composite Longbow (+2 Str)**
> 
> **Without Mounted Archery:**
> - Base attack: +6
> - Dexterity: +4
> - Bow bonus: +2
> - **Moving penalty**: -4
> - **Total**: +8 to hit
> 
> **With Mounted Archery:**
> - Base attack: +6
> - Dexterity: +4
> - Bow bonus: +2
> - **Moving penalty**: -2
> - **Total**: +10 to hit
> 
> **Impact**: +2 to hit = ~10% better chance to hit = ~15% more damage over time

**Build Recommendations:**
- **Essential for**: ALL mounted archer builds
- **Skip if**: You don't use ranged weapons while mounted
- **Priority**: Take early (level 1 or 3)
- **Synergy**: Rangers, Scouts, DEX-focused fighters

**Mounted Archer Tactics:**

**Hit-and-Run Archery:**
> **Round 1**:
> - Move 60 ft. toward enemy
> - Shoot with -2 penalty
> - Stay mobile, hard to target

> **Round 2**:
> - Circle around (60 ft. movement)
> - Shoot again with -2 penalty
> - Maintain distance from melee enemies

**Kiting Strategy:**
> - Enemy charges you
> - You move 60 ft. away (Light Horse speed)
> - Shoot enemy with -2 penalty
> - Enemy can't reach you (only 30 ft. movement)
> - Repeat until enemy is full of arrows

**Versatile Combatant:**
> - **Turn 1**: Shoot from horseback (ranged)
> - **Turn 2**: Charge with lance (melee)

> - **Turn 4**: Shoot while repositioning

### Cover Bonus (AC +4)

**Detailed coverage in Section 4 "Ride Skill DC 10: Cover"**

**Quick Reference:**
- **DC**: 10
- **Action**: Immediate action (reaction to being attacked)
- **Success**: +4 cover bonus to AC
- **Limitation**: Can't attack while using cover

**How It Works:**
1. Enemy declares attack against you
2. You use immediate action to declare Cover
3. Make DC 10 Ride check
4. Success: +4 AC applies to that attack
5. You hang alongside mount, using it for cover

**Tactical Usage:**

**Best Against:**
✅ Ranged attacks (arrows, spells)
✅ High attack rolls (when normal AC isn't enough)
✅ Critical hit confirmations (cover bonus may prevent crit)
✅ When you don't need to attack that round

**Don't Use Against:**
❌ Weak attacks you'd probably dodge anyway
❌ When you need to make attacks of opportunity
❌ Area effects (cover doesn't help vs. fireballs)

**Example:**
> **Ranger vs. Enemy Sniper**
> - Sniper attacks: +12 to hit vs Ranger's AC 16
> - Roll: 18 + 12 = 30 (definitely hits!)
> - Ranger uses Cover: DC 10 Ride check
> - Roll: 8 + 8 = 16 ✅ **Success!**
> - AC becomes 20 (16 + 4 cover)
> - 30 vs AC 20 still hits, but...
> - Damage reduced, crit less likely
> 
> **Alternative**: If sniper rolled 16 + 12 = 28
> - With cover: 28 vs AC 20 still hits
> - Without cover: 28 vs AC 16 also hits
> - Cover made no difference this time

**Synergy with Height Advantage:**
```
Base AC: 17
+ Height advantage: +1 (vs. enemies on foot)
+ Cover: +4 (immediate action)
= Total AC: 22

This can turn near-certain hits into misses!
```

### Spur Mount for Extra Speed

**Detailed coverage in Section 4 "Ride Skill DC 15: Spur Mount"**

**Quick Reference:**
- **DC**: 15
- **Benefit**: +10 feet to mount's speed for 1 round
- **Cost**: 1 point of damage to mount
- **Limitation**: Can't spur exhausted mount

**When to Use:**
✅ Chasing fleeing enemy (need those extra 10 ft. to reach)
✅ Escaping dangerous situation (every foot counts)
✅ Reaching ally in need (get there one round earlier)
✅ Positioning for critical charge (need perfect distance)

**When NOT to Use:**
❌ Mount already wounded severely (1 HP matters)
❌ Long journey ahead (mount needs to stay fresh)
❌ You'd reach target without spurring (unnecessary damage)
❌ Mount is exhausted (impossible to spur)

**Example:**
> **Paladin chasing fleeing goblin**
> - Goblin: 40 ft. away, running (120 ft. this turn)
> - Heavy Warhorse: 50 ft. speed (100 ft. charge)
> - **Problem**: Can't reach goblin (only 100 ft. vs 120 ft.)
> 
> **Solution**: Spur Mount
> - DC 15 Ride check
> - Roll: 11 + 9 = 20 ✅ **Success!**
> - Speed becomes 60 ft. (charge 120 ft.)
> - Reaches goblin!
> - Mount takes 1 damage (acceptable trade)
> 
> **Result**: Charges goblin, Spirited Charge lance attack, 33 damage, goblin dead

**Damage Consideration:**
```
Heavy Warhorse HP: 45 average
Spur damage: 1 HP
Percentage: 2.2% of total HP

Unless mount is already critically wounded, 1 HP is negligible cost for +10 ft. speed.
```

**Strategic Situations:**

**Scenario 1: Every Foot Matters**
> - Ally surrounded, 65 ft. away
> - Mount speed: 50 ft. (can't reach)
> - Spur mount: 60 ft. (can reach!)
> - Result: Save ally's life for 1 HP

**Scenario 2: Charge Optimization**
> - Enemy is 110 ft. away
> - Mount charge range: 100 ft. (can't charge)
> - Spur mount: 120 ft. charge range (can charge!)
> - Result: Spirited Charge instead of normal attack

**Scenario 3: Pursuit**
> - Enemy runs 80 ft. away
> - Mount speed: 50 ft. (can't catch)
> - Spur mount: 60 ft. (still can't catch, but closer)
> - Next round: Will be in charge range
> - Result: One round earlier engagement

### Protecting Your Mount with Mounted Combat Feat

**Comprehensive coverage in Section 8 "Mounted Combat Feat"**

**Quick Reference:**
- **Feat**: Mounted Combat (prerequisite: Ride 1 rank)
- **Benefit**: Once per round, negate one hit against mount
- **Mechanic**: Ride check (DC = enemy's attack roll)
- **Success**: Hit negated, mount takes 0 damage
- **Failure**: Hit connects normally

**Priority System:**
```
Attacks on Mount (Priority Order):
1. Critical hits → Always try to negate
2. High damage rolls → Worth negating (10+ damage)
3. Killing blows → Definitely negate if possible
4. Regular hits → Let mount take damage if minor

Example:
- Goblin hits mount: 4 damage → Let it hit
- Ogre hits mount: 18 damage → Negate with Mounted Combat!
```

**Example:**
> **Level 5 Fighter on Heavy Warhorse (30 HP)**
> 
> **Round 1**:
> - Orc attacks mount: Roll 16 vs AC 14, hit! (1d12+4 = 10 damage)
> - Fighter: "Not worth negating" → Mount takes 10 damage (30 → 20 HP)
> 
> **Round 2**:
> - Ogre attacks mount: Roll 22 vs AC 14, hit! (2d8+10 = 18 damage)
> - Fighter uses Mounted Combat: DC 22 Ride check
> - Fighter's Ride: +9
> - Roll: 14 + 9 = 23 ✅ **Success!**
> - Hit negated, mount still at 20 HP
> 
> **Round 2 (continued)**:
> - Orc attacks mount again: Roll 15 vs AC 14, hit! (8 damage)
> - Fighter already used Mounted Combat this round
> - Can't negate second hit
> - Mount takes 8 damage (20 → 12 HP)
> 
> **Analysis**: 
> - Without Mounted Combat: Mount would be at 2 HP (critically wounded)
> - With Mounted Combat: Mount at 12 HP (still functional)

**Mount HP Management:**

**Healthy Mount (75%+ HP):**
- Let minor hits connect (save Mounted Combat for big threats)
- Focus on offense
- Don't worry too much about chip damage

**Wounded Mount (25-75% HP):**
- Start negating medium hits (8+ damage)
- Be more conservative
- Consider positioning to reduce incoming damage

**Critical Mount (< 25% HP):**
- Negate any hit you can successfully roll
- Consider dismounting and fighting on foot
- Use healing if available

**When Your Mount Dies:**

**Immediate Effects:**
1. You fall (make DC 15 Soft Fall check)
2. Success: Land on feet
3. Failure: Fall prone
4. Mount's body becomes difficult terrain
5. You're now on foot (reduced mobility)

**Tactical Response:**
```
If Mount Dies:
1. Soft Fall check (DC 15) → Try to land gracefully
2. Assess situation:
   - Surrounded? → Fight defensively, try to escape
   - Winning? → Continue fighting on foot
   - Losing? → Withdraw, regroup
3. Consider backup mount (if you brought spare)
4. Adapt tactics to foot combat
```

**Prevention:**
- Take Mounted Combat feat (essential)
- Max out Ride skill (improves negation success rate)
- Position mount away from highest threats
- Use terrain for mount protection
- Have healing ready (potions, spells, wands)

> 🛡️ **See Section 8 "Mounted Combat" for full tactical analysis**

## 12. Tips & Best Practices

### When to Use Mounted Combat vs Foot Combat

**Mounted Combat Excels In:**

✅ **Open Terrain**
- Plains, grasslands, valleys, beaches
- Reason: Mobility advantage, no obstacles
- Example: Charging across battlefield, kiting enemies

✅ **Outdoor Encounters**
- Wilderness, roads, farmland, deserts
- Reason: Space for charges, mounted maneuvers
- Example: Ambushing travellers, pursuing bandits

✅ **Large Battlefields**
- Mass combat, army battles, sieges
- Reason: Mounted units dominate large-scale warfare
- Example: Cavalry charges, flanking maneuvers

✅ **Pursuit/Escape Situations**
- Chasing fleeing enemies, escaping overwhelming odds
- Reason: Speed advantage (50-60 ft. vs. 30 ft. on foot)
- Example: Catching fleeing orcs, retreating from giants

✅ **Fighting Weaker/Slower Enemies**
- Goblins, kobolds, zombies, slow creatures
- Reason: Superior mobility lets you control engagement
- Example: Kiting zombies, charging and retreating from goblins

**Foot Combat Excels In:**

✅ **Dungeons and Caves**
- Narrow corridors, low ceilings, tight spaces
- Reason: Mount can't fit or maneuver
- Example: 10 ft. wide dungeon hallway (mount is Large = 10 ft. square, barely fits!)

✅ **Dense Forests**
- Thick trees, underbrush, difficult terrain
- Reason: Can't charge through obstacles
- Example: Fighting druids in deep woods

✅ **Urban Combat**
- Inside buildings, narrow streets, stairs
- Reason: Mount can't climb stairs or enter buildings
- Example: Fighting in tavern, climbing guard tower

✅ **Underwater/Flying Combat**
- Aquatic combat, aerial enemies
- Reason: Mount can't swim or fly (usually)
- Example: Fighting in underwater cave, vs. harpies

✅ **Fighting Large/Tall Enemies**
- Giants, dragons, huge creatures
- Reason: Lose height advantage (+1 AC bonus doesn't apply)
- Example: Giant is taller than you even when mounted

**Hybrid Approach (Best Strategy):**

🐎 **Bring Mount to Outdoor Locations**
- Leave mount outside dungeon entrance
- Have ally guard mount or tie it securely
- Enter dungeon on foot
- Return to mount for outdoor travel/combat

**Example:**
> Party travels overland on mounts (fast travel)
> Reaches dungeon entrance
> Dismount and leave horses with hired guard
> Explore dungeon on foot
> Exit dungeon, remount, continue journey

**Tactical Flexibility:**
```
Scenario: Ambushed on road by bandits

Round 1-3: Mounted combat (open road, use charges and mobility)

Bandits flee into forest:
Round 4: Dismount, pursue on foot (dense trees block mounts)

Bandits eliminated, return to road:
Round 5: Remount, continue journey
```

## 13. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Forgetting to Control Mount (DC 5)

**Mistake:**
You attack with a two-handed weapon or bow, forgetting to make DC 5 Ride check to guide with knees.

**Consequence:**
- DM says: "You lose control of your mount, you can't use both hands."
- Must choose: Hold reins (one hand) OR drop reins (uncontrolled mount)
- Action wasted, can't attack effectively

**Example:**
> **Fighter with greatsword attacks ogre**
> - Player: "I attack with my greatsword!"
> - DM: "Did you make your DC 5 Ride check to guide with knees?"
> - Player: "Oh... I forgot!"
> - DM: "You lose control trying to use both hands. Your mount moves unpredictably. No attack this round."

**How to Avoid:**
✅ Make DC 5 check **at the start of your turn** if using two-handed weapon/bow
✅ Invest in high Ride bonus (+5 or more) so you auto-succeed
✅ Announce: "I guide with knees" every turn (reminder to yourself and DM)
✅ With +5 Ride bonus, only fail on natural 1 (5% chance)

---

### 2. Using Regular Horse in Combat (Not War-Trained)

**Mistake:**
You bring a regular Light Horse (75 gp) into combat instead of Light Warhorse (150 gp).

**Consequence:**
- Horse panics when combat starts (DC 20 Ride check to control each round)
- If check fails: Horse flees 4× speed (240 ft.!) away from battle
- You're carried away from fight, wasting rounds trying to regain control
- Horse won't attack even if controlled (not trained)

**Example:**
> **Wizard on regular Light Horse vs. bandits**
> - Combat starts, bandits charge
> - Wizard must make DC 20 Ride check: Roll 8 + 2 = 10 ❌ **FAIL!**
> - Horse panics and runs 240 ft. away
> - Wizard's turn wasted, too far to cast spells
> - Party fights 1 member short
> - Wizard spends 2 more rounds trying to regain control and return
> - By the time Wizard returns, fight is over

**How to Avoid:**
✅ **ALWAYS buy war-trained mounts for combat characters** (50-150 gp more is worth it!)
✅ Regular horses: Only for non-combat travel (wizards, clerics who stay in backline)
✅ Check mount description: "War-trained?" = essential for combat
✅ If you have regular horse and combat starts: Stay far away, dismount, fight on foot

**Cost Comparison:**
```
Regular Light Horse: 75 gp
Light Warhorse: 150 gp
Difference: 75 gp

Is 75 gp worth reliable combat performance? ABSOLUTELY YES!
```

### 3. Wasting Mounted Combat Feat Use

**Mistake:**
You use Mounted Combat feat to negate a minor hit (3-5 damage), then a big hit comes and you can't negate it (already used this round).

**Consequence:**
- Minor hit negated: Mount saves 3 damage
- Major hit connects: Mount takes 18 damage (could have been negated!)
- Poor resource management leads to mount death

**Example:**
> **Paladin's warhorse vs. goblins and ogre**
> - Goblin attacks warhorse: 4 damage
> - Paladin: "I use Mounted Combat to negate!"
> - Ride check DC 12: Success, 4 damage negated
> 
> **Later that round:**
> - Ogre attacks warhorse: 18 damage (massive hit!)
> - Paladin: "I use Mounted Combat again!"
> - DM: "You already used it this round. Mount takes 18 damage."
> - Warhorse HP: 30 → 12 (critically wounded)
> 
> **Result:** Saved 4 damage, lost 18 damage = Net loss of 14 damage

**How to Avoid:**
✅ **Prioritize high-damage attacks**: Only negate hits of 10+ damage
✅ **Watch enemy attack bonuses**: High-attack enemies = more likely to hit = save for them
✅ **Critical hits**: ALWAYS negate critical hits (double damage is huge)
✅ **Mount HP management**: If mount below 50% HP, negate even medium hits

**Priority System:**
```
1st Priority: Critical hits (2x damage)
2nd Priority: High damage (15+ damage)
3rd Priority: Medium damage when mount wounded (10-14 damage)
4th Priority: Low damage (5-9 damage) only if mount critical
Never: Minor hits (1-4 damage) unless mount at 5 HP or less
```

### 4. Not Investing in Ride Skill

**Mistake:**
You take mounted combat feats but only have +2 Ride bonus.

**Consequence:**
- DC 10 checks (control warhorse): 60% success (need 8+) = Often fail
- DC 15 checks (soft fall, spur): 35% success = Rarely succeed
- DC 20 checks (fast mount): 10% success = Almost never succeed

**Example:**

**How to Avoid:**
✅ **Invest heavily in Ride**: Max ranks every level
✅ **Minimum +8 bonus** for reliable DC 10 checks (95% success)
✅ **Target +12-15** for DC 15 checks (reliable soft fall, spur)
✅ **Class skill**: Make sure Ride is a class skill (+3 bonus)
✅ **Skill Focus (Ride)**: +3 bonus (worthwhile feat for mounted specialists)

- DC 10: Auto

### Additional Common Mistakes

**7. Charging Without Clear Path**

**Mistake:** You declare charge but path has obstacles (allies, furniture, trees).

**Consequence:** Charge fails, you waste full-round action and can't attack.

**How to Avoid:**
- ✅ Check path before declaring charge
- ✅ Ask DM: "Is there a clear path to charge?"
- ✅ Ready action instead if path unclear

**8. Using Lance Adjacent to Enemy**

**Mistake:** Enemy closes to melee, you try to attack with lance (reach weapon).

**Consequence:** Can't attack adjacent enemies with reach weapon, wasted turn.

**How to Avoid:**
- ✅ Carry backup weapon (longsword, mace)
- ✅ Draw backup weapon when enemy gets adjacent
- ✅ Or use mount's attacks (DC 10 Ride check)

**9. Charging Alone Into Enemy Group**

**Mistake:** You charge 1 vs 5 enemies, get surrounded and killed.

**Consequence:** Devastating opening charge, then death from multiple attacks.

**How to Avoid:**
- ✅ Use Ride-By Attack feat to charge past and escape
- ✅ Coordinate with party (don't charge alone)
- ✅ Target isolated enemies, not groups

---

**10. Not Protecting Mount After Combat**

**Mistake:** After battle, mount is at 8/30 HP, you don't heal it, next battle it dies immediately.

**Consequence:** Mount dies in second fight, you're on foot and vulnerable.

**How to Avoid:**
- ✅ Heal mount between combats (potions, spells)
- ✅ Rest to recover HP (mounts recover 1 HP/day naturally)
- ✅ Consider replacing mount if critically wounded

### Damage Calculation Reference

**Standard Charge Damage:**
```
Base Damage: Weapon die + Strength modifier + other bonuses
Lance Charge: (Base Damage) × 2
Spirited Charge (normal weapon): (Base Damage) × 2
Spirited Charge (lance): (Base Damage) × 3
```

**Example:**
```
Fighter with Lance, Strength 16, Power Attack -3, +1 Lance, Level 6

Base Damage:
- Lance: 1d8

= 1d8+11

Average: 4.5 + 11 = 15.5

Spirited Charge (lance): 15.5 × 3 = 46.5 damage
```

**Power Attack Ratios:**
- **One-handed**: 1:1 (trade -1 attack for +1 damage)
- **Two-handed**: 1:2 (trade -1 attack for +2 damage)

## 16. Troubleshooting

### Q: My mount won't attack. Why?

**A:** Check these issues:

1. **Is your mount war-trained?**
   - Regular horses (Light Horse, Heavy Horse, Pony) are NOT war-trained
   - They will NOT attack in combat
   - **Solution**: Buy war-trained mount (warhorse, war pony)

2. **Did you make your DC 10 Ride check?**
   - War-trained mounts require DC 10 Ride check to command
   - If you fail, mount doesn't attack that round
   - **Solution**: Invest in Ride skill (+8 or higher for 95% success)

3. **Is your mount controlled or uncontrolled?**
   - Uncontrolled mounts act on their own initiative and don't follow commands
   - **Solution**: Keep mount controlled (make Ride checks each round)

4. **Are you using a move or standard action to command?**
   - Commanding mount to attack is FREE action (with DC 10 Ride check)
   - You don't need to spend actions
   - **Solution**: Just make the DC 10 check during your turn

**Example Fix:**
> **Problem**: "My Light Warhorse isn't attacking!"
> 
> **Player**: "I attack with my longsword."
> **DM**: "What about your mount?"
> **Player**: "Oh! I make DC 10 Ride check: 1d20+8 = 15, success!"
> **DM**: "Your warhorse attacks with bite and hooves!"

### Q: I fell off my mount. What happened?

**A:** Several reasons you might fall:

1. **Failed fast mount/dismount (DC 20 check)**
   - If you fail DC 20 Ride check for fast mount/dismount, you fall prone
   - **Solution**: Only attempt fast mount if you have +10 or higher Ride bonus

2. **Failed stay in saddle (DC 5 check)**
   - When mount rears, jumps, or moves suddenly, you make DC 5 Ride check
   - **Solution**: Invest in at least +5 Ride bonus (95% success rate)

3. **You were knocked off by attack**
   - Some attacks (bull rush, trip, special abilities) knock you off mount
   - **Soft Fall (DC 15)**: If you succeed, land on feet; if fail, fall prone
   - **Solution**: Invest in Ride skill for reliable Soft Fall checks

4. **Your mount was killed**
   - When mount dies beneath you, you fall
   - Make DC 15 Soft Fall check to land gracefully
   - **Solution**: Protect mount with Mounted Combat feat

**What to Do After Falling:**

```
If you landed on feet (Soft Fall success):
- You're standing, can act normally
- Mount is dead or nearby
- Consider remounting or fighting on foot

If you fell prone (Soft Fall failure):
- -4 AC until you stand
- Enemies get +4 to attack you
- Standing provokes AoO
- **Immediate action**: Stand up (move action) or crawl to safety
```

### Q: Can't mount my horse. What's wrong?

**A:** Check these size compatibility issues:

1. **Are you the right size for your mount?**
   - You must be **at least one size smaller** than mount
   - Medium rider → needs Large mount (any horse)
   - Small rider → needs Medium mount (pony, riding dog)
   - **Solution**: Buy appropriate-sized mount for your character

2. **Is the mount willing?**
   - Wild or hostile mounts won't let you mount
   - **Solution**: Tame mount (Handle Animal check) or buy trained mount

3. **Are you adjacent to the mount?**
   - You must be in a square adjacent to mount
   - **Solution**: Move next to mount first

4. **Is the mount prone?**
   - Can't mount a prone creature
   - **Solution**: Wait for mount to stand, or help it stand

**Size Chart:**
| Your Size | Mount Size | Examples |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Small** (Halfling, Gnome) | **Medium** | Pony, War Pony, Riding Dog |
| **Medium** (Human, Elf, Dwarf) | **Large** | Any Horse or Warhorse |
| **Large** (Ogre, Giant) | **Huge** | Dire Wolf, Elephant |

### Q: Why can't I charge?

**A:** Charges have specific requirements. Check these:

1. **Is there a clear, straight path to target?**
   - Must charge in straight or nearly straight line
   - Obstacles block charges: allies, furniture, trees, walls
   - **Solution**: Position for clear charge lane or move closer first

2. **Is target within charge range?**
   - Charge range = **double mount's speed**
   - Light Warhorse (60 ft.) = 120 ft. charge range
   - Heavy Warhorse (50 ft.) = 100 ft. charge range
   - **Solution**: Get within range or spur mount (+10 ft. for 1 round)

3. **Is the terrain difficult?**
   - Can't charge through difficult terrain (swamp, dense forest, rubble)
   - **Solution**: Find clear ground or dismount and fight on foot

4. **Do you have line of sight?**
   - Must see target to charge
   - **Solution**: Move to position with clear view

5. **Did you already take another action?**
   - Charge is **full-round action** (your whole turn)
   - If you already moved or attacked, can't charge
   - **Solution**: Plan charge at start of turn

**Example:**
> **Setup**: Fighter wants to charge orc 80 ft. away
> 
> **Check 1**: Clear path? ✅ Yes (open field)
> **Check 2**: Within range? ✅ Yes (80 ft. < 100 ft. charge range)
> **Check 3**: Difficult terrain? ✅ No (clear ground)
> **Check 4**: Line of sight? ✅ Yes (can see orc)
> **Check 5**: Full-round action available? ✅ Yes (start of turn)
> 
> **Result**: Charge succeeds!

### Q: My mount keeps panicking and running away. How do I stop this?

**A:** Your mount isn't war-trained!

**Problem**: Regular mounts (Light Horse, Heavy Horse, Pony) panic in combat
- Require DC 20 Ride check EACH ROUND to maintain control
- If you fail: Mount flees 4× speed away from danger (240 ft. for Light Horse!)
- This happens because regular mounts aren't trained for combat

**Solution 1: Buy War-Trained Mount (BEST SOLUTION)**
```
Replace regular mount with war-trained version:
- Regular Light Horse (75 gp) → Light Warhorse (150 gp)
- Regular Heavy Horse (200 gp) → Heavy Warhorse (400 gp)
- Regular Pony (30 gp) → War Pony (100 gp)

War-trained mounts:
- Won't panic in combat
- Only DC 10 Ride check to control (much easier!)
- Can attack enemies when commanded
```

**Solution 2: Train Your Existing Mount**
```
Use Handle Animal skill to train mount for combat:
- Requires: Animal Trainer (Handle Animal ranks)
- Time: 1 week of training
- Cost: Trainer fee (~50-150 gp)
- DC 20 Handle Animal check
- **Warning**: Time-consuming, may not be worth it vs. buying new mount
```

**Solution 3: Keep Mount Away from Combat**
```
If you're stuck with regular mount:
- Leave mount far from battle (100+ ft. away)
- Tie mount securely to tree/post
- Fight on foot
- Return to mount after combat
- Use mount for travel only, not combat
```

**Example:**
> **Wizard on regular Light Horse**
> 
> **Before Fix**:
> - Combat starts 60 ft. from enemies
> - Horse panics (DC 20 Ride check: 5 + 2 = 7, FAIL!)
> - Horse flees 240 ft., carrying wizard away
> - Wizard's turn wasted, party down one caster
> 
> **After Fix** (bought Light Warhorse):
> - Combat starts 60 ft. from enemies
> - Warhorse stays calm (war-trained)
> - DC 10 Ride check: 8 + 2 = 10, SUCCESS
> - Wizard casts fireball from horseback
> - Warhorse stays in position, ready for next turn

> ⚠️ **ALWAYS buy war-trained mounts for combat characters!** The 75-150 gp difference is absolutely worth it.

### Q: How do I know if I should use mounted combat in this fight?

**A:** Use this decision flowchart:

```
START: Should I fight mounted?

Is the terrain suitable for mounted combat?
├─ YES: Open field, plains, roads → Continue
└─ NO: Dungeon, dense forest, indoors → FIGHT ON FOOT

Is there space for charges (60+ ft. clear lanes)?
├─ YES: Can charge → STRONG RECOMMEND MOUNTED
└─ NO: Enemies within 20 ft. → Continue

Is my mount healthy (above 75% HP)?
├─ YES: Mount can take hits → Continue
└─ NO: Mount wounded → CONSIDER DISMOUNTING

Do I have Mounted Combat feat to protect mount?
├─ YES: Can negate hits → RECOMMEND MOUNTED
└─ NO: Mount vulnerable → RISKY

Are enemies numerous and surrounding?
├─ YES: Risk of being surrounded → CAUTIOUS
└─ NO: 1-3 enemies, manageable → RECOMMEND MOUNTED

FINAL DECISION:
→ FIGHT MOUNTED if:
  ✅ Open terrain
  ✅ Space for charges
  ✅ Mount healthy
  ✅ Have Mounted Combat feat
  ✅ Not surrounded

→ FIGHT ON FOOT if:
  ✅ Indoor/dungeon
  ✅ Mount wounded badly
  ✅ Surrounded by enemies
  ✅ Difficult terrain
```

**Example Scenarios:**

**Scenario 1: Open Plains vs 3 Orcs (80 ft. away)**
- Terrain: ✅ Open
- Charge space: ✅ Yes
- Mount HP: ✅ 100%
- Feat: ✅ Have Mounted Combat
- Enemies: ✅ Only 3
- **Decision: FIGHT MOUNTED**

**Scenario 2: Dungeon Corridor vs 5 Goblins (15 ft. away)**
- Terrain: ❌ Dungeon, 10 ft. wide
- Charge space: ❌ No room
- Mount HP: ✅ 100%
- Feat: ✅ Have Mounted Combat
- Enemies: ⚠️ 5 (risky)
- **Decision: FIGHT ON FOOT** (mount can barely fit, can't charge)

**Scenario 3: Forest Clearing vs 1 Ogre (30 ft. away)**
- Terrain: ⚠️ Light forest (trees)
- Charge space: ⚠️ Limited
- Mount HP: ⚠️ 40% (wounded)
- Feat: ✅ Have Mounted Combat
- Enemies: ✅ Only 1
- **Decision: MARGINAL** (could go either way; if mount dies, you're in trouble)

### Q: My lance can't attack the enemy next to me. What do I do?

**A:** Lance is a reach weapon (10 ft. reach), can't attack adjacent enemies.

**Solutions:**

**Option 1: Draw Backup Weapon (RECOMMENDED)**
```
Action Breakdown:
- Move action: Sheathe lance (or drop it as free action)
- Move action: Draw longsword/mace
- Wait until next turn to attack with backup weapon

OR (with BAB +1):
- Free action: Sheathe lance
- Move action: Draw backup weapon
- Standard action: Attack this turn!
```

**Option 2: Use Mount's Attacks**
```
- You can't attack (lance can't reach)
- But mount can still attack!
- Make DC 10 Ride check
- Mount gets bite + 2 hooves against adjacent enemy
- Better than nothing!
```

**Option 3: 5-Foot Step Back**
```
- 5-foot step away from enemy (doesn't provoke AoO)
- Now enemy is 5 ft. away (still can't attack, lance needs 10 ft.)
- Next round: Move 5 more ft. away (now 10 ft. total)
- Attack with lance at 10 ft. reach

Problem: Takes 2 rounds to reposition, enemy can close distance
```

**Option 4: Charge Away and Return**
```
- Withdraw (move action, no AoO)
- Move 50 ft. away from enemy
- Next round: Charge back (100 ft. charge range)
- Attack at 10 ft. reach during charge
- Bonus: Charge damage (+2 attack, Spirited Charge multiplier!)

Best option if you have space!
```

**Recommended Loadout:**
```
Primary Weapon: Lance (reach, devastating charges)
Backup Weapon: Longsword or Mace (adjacent enemies)

Equipment Investment:
- Lance: 10 gp
- Longsword: 15 gp
- Total: 25 gp for complete coverage

Worth it? ABSOLUTELY!
```

**Example:**
> **Round 1**: Paladin charges orc with lance (48 damage, orc dies)
> **Round 2**: Goblin closes to adjacent
> **Paladin's options**:
> - Option A: Draw longsword, wait until next turn (SLOW)
> - Option B: Mount attacks goblin (DC 10 check, 3 attacks) (GOOD)
> - Option C: Withdraw 50 ft., next turn charge back (BEST if space available!)
> 
> **Paladin chooses Option C**:
> - Withdraws 50 ft.
> - Round 3: Charges goblin with lance (39 damage, goblin obliterated!)

> 💡 **Always carry a backup melee weapon when using lance!**
