# Enemy AI: where it stands today

This is the reference for planning and building deeper enemy AI. It describes how a non-player combatant takes its turn today, which parts of the 3.5e combat option space it can use, where the code deviates from the PHB, DMG and MM, and how to extend it. How the AI fits into the rest of the code is summarised in [architecture/ai-encounters-ui.md](../architecture/ai-encounters-ui.md); bugs live in [issues/AI.md](../issues/AI.md) and the other `issues/` files and are cited here by ID. Creature content (which monsters have which abilities) is in [CREATURES.md](CREATURES.md); spell content is in [SPELLS_AND_METAMAGIC.md](SPELLS_AND_METAMAGIC.md). The tactics inventory in [tactics/README.md](tactics/README.md) lists every action, spell and creature ability in the level 1-8 scope with its AI status and a tactical model, and proposes the action-catalog schema and backlog. For a concrete request ("make goblins flank", "make enemies drink potions"), start at [section 11.8](#118-request-playbooks), which maps likely requests to entry points, existing code, blockers and the issues to fix first.

Conventions:

- Paths are relative to `Assets/Scripts/` unless they start with `docs/`. Line numbers are from commit 0dd8e76 and drift; search for the method name.
- Everything here was established by reading code. **Nothing was verified in Play mode** unless a line says so. "Inferred" marks a behaviour worked out by tracing several methods.
- Defects are cited by their issue ID (`AI-nnn`, `CMB-nnn` and so on; see `docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`). The ones found while writing this doc are summarised in [section 10.2](#102-issues-found-while-writing-this-doc).
- Rules citations give book and chapter. Check the book before filing a rules deviation.

## 1. Summary

**What the AI does today.**

- Every non-controllable combatant (enemy NPCs, enemy-side summons, AI-controlled swarms) runs `AIService.ExecuteNPCTurn`, a coroutine that applies a fixed chain of gates and then hands the turn to one of six hand-written routines: AggressiveMelee, RangedKiter, DefensiveMelee, Dragon, Swarm, Healer, plus a separate summon routine in `GameManager`.
- It picks a target by scoring every visible enemy (profile score plus a large visibility term), moves with a single move action to the best-scoring reachable cell, then charges, maneuvers, or makes a single or full attack.
- It can charge (with pounce), trip, disarm, grapple (including a weighted-random in-grapple routine), coup de grace, take a 5-foot step in two narrow cases, withdraw when badly hurt (DefensiveMelee only), fire breath weapons with real cone/line positioning (Dragon profile only), emit auras, cast single-target spells (Healer, Dragon and RangedKiter routines only), track invisible enemies by last-known position and Listen checks, and follow forced behaviour for Confused, Charmed and Frightened.

**Biggest limitations.**

1. **Narrow action set.** No double move, run, total defense (except the cornered panicked creature's, batch 13 step 4), ready, delay, Power Attack, Combat Expertise, two-weapon fighting, Aid Another and waking an ally (catalog executors the AI can run since batch 13 step 13, D6, but never chooses; batch 14), dropping prone or crawling (catalog executors the AI can run since batch 13 step 12, D5, but never chooses; batch 14), weapon switching, item use, the activated domain powers (a catalog executor the AI can run since batch 13 step 16 but never chooses; batch 14), spontaneous cure/inflict conversion, area spells or metamagic (section 9). Class abilities the AI does use: Flurry of Blows (CHR-003), Smite, Lay on Hands and Wild Shape (CHR-020, 2026-10-14), bardic music (CHR-026, 2026-10-15), and since batch 13 step 16 (2026-10-29) turn undead and rage (11.8.7; design choice C6: turn when its best check could affect the nearest undead, rage at turn start with a foe within reach or charge range). No PC-only catalog entry is left: every action the PC panel offers runs through a shared executor the AI runner can reach.
2. **Asymmetries that favour NPCs.** Since batch 13 step 18 (2026-10-30, D11) the breath weapon is a standard action for every breather, and the breath, spittle, engulf and ranged specials are shared catalog entries a controllable creature can take too (AI-040 fixed; the triggers and auras are CRE-039); since batch 12 step 10 every save of either side goes through one roll (`SaveRollRules`: natural 1 and 20, Luck, countersong) and a monster's ranged touch attack uses the spell ranged touch terms (AI-006 fixed); since batch 11 step 13 a silenced NPC, like a PC, cannot cast a verbal spell (the cast core's Legality stage, and the AI's spell choice skips such a spell). Damage from spells, breath and specials is shared since 2026-10-09 (`GameManager.DealDamage`, SPL-004). Casting (7.6), movement and maneuver attacks of opportunity (4.5) use the same helpers for PCs and NPCs.
3. **Asymmetries that cripple NPCs.** Prone NPCs, charmed ones included, stand at turn start, and since batch 13 step 12 (2026-10-29, D5, CMB-074 fixed) a prone confused one stands when its roll lets it act and it cannot already attack the nearest creature from the ground, and when it rolls flee (revised in the step 12 review); the AI never chooses to crawl (batch 14). Since batch 12 step 7 (2026-10-28, decision D4) panicked and turned NPCs flee, pinned ones try to escape and nauseated ones take their single move action, as PCs do. Breath weapons fire only for the Dragon profile (AI-033); they recharge as their MM entries say since batch 12 step 6 (D14). Almost no monster can actually cast: the lich has no prepared spells (SPL-015), the vampire is not a spellcaster (CRE-030), class-levelled DMG spawns have no spell lists (ENC-021) and creature spell-like abilities do not exist (CRE-015, CRE-017).
4. **No decision layer.** Each routine is a fixed script. There is no comparison of the expected value of a spell, a breath, a full attack or a maneuver; `SelectBestAction` computes six results but its callers act only on `Charge` (AI-027). Profiles that set Trip trip every standing target in reach with their first attack, with no odds check (AI-035); a per-turn stopgap (AI-060) only stops them chaining maneuvers or retrying a failed one.
5. **No group play.** Every NPC scores alone. There is no focus fire, flank pairing, protection of casters, morale or retreat policy (AI-010).

**Mental model.** Think of an NPC's AI as three stacked layers. The **turn pipeline** (`ExecuteNPCTurn`) owns the order of operations and the condition takeovers. The **tactical routine** (chosen by `NPCAIBehavior`, the profile class, or the summon/swarm status) owns the shape of the turn: approach-then-hit, cast-then-kite, breathe-then-retreat. The **profile** (`AIProfile` subclass chosen by `NPCAIProfileArchetype`) only answers questions the routine asks: how good is this target, should I charge, which maneuver, which spell. Spell choice is a fourth, mostly independent scorer (`AISpellcastingStrategist`). Most resolution (attacks, breath, casting) is done by `GameManager` partials that `AIService` reaches through about 40 `*ForAI` wrappers. To change *what* an NPC can do you usually edit a routine and a `GameManager` executor; to change *how it chooses* you edit a profile or the strategist.

### 2.4 Where configuration comes from

Per-NPC AI state lives in three places:

- `NPCDefinition` fields (above). `InitializeNPCFromDefinition` copies them: `npc.aiProfile = BuildRuntimeAIProfile(def, stats)` (the type from `RuntimeAIProfileTypeFor`, batch 13 step 8), `EnemyUseCoupDeGraceOverride` (855), `PriorityTargetName` (856).
- `GameManager._npcAIBehaviors`, a list index-parallel to `NPCs` and read by `GetNPCBehaviorForAI` (AI-015, CRE-006).
- One `AIProfile` instance per creature from `GameManager.CreateRuntimeAIProfile<T>()` (`AIProfile.CreateRuntime<T>()`: `CreateInstance`, then the virtual `ApplyRuntimeDefaults`, which writes the type's tuning and its `ProfileName`), tracked so that the GameManager destroys it: a replaced profile at once, a reused slot's old one at the respawn, a summon's once it is despawned (the despawn loops and the combat reset; batch 13 step 2, AI-017). `OnEnable` writes no tuning, so a profile `.asset` would keep its Inspector values; there are no profile `.asset` files. A profile may hold per-turn state (the dragon's breath plan) because no two creatures share an instance; the scenario harness and the static suites call `AIProfile.CreateRuntime` directly.

## 3. The NPC turn pipeline

### 3.1 From initiative to the AI

1. `TurnService.StartCombat` rolls initiative (d20 + modifier; ties by modifier, then random) and fires `OnTurnStarted` for each actor. `AISpellcastingStrategist.ResetCombatState` runs right after (GameManager.cs:3735).
2. `GameManager.OnTurnStarted` (3764) sends controllable characters to `StartPCTurn` and everything else to `SingleNPCTurnFromInitiative` (3800). `IsPC` means controllable (CORE-014).
3. `SingleNPCTurnFromInitiative` (NPCTurns.cs:35) sets the phase and UI, expires Aid Another bonuses, handles Flaming Sphere, then calls **`ShouldSkipTurnDueToHPState`**. If that is true it logs "X is <reason> and cannot act" and calls `NextInitiativeTurn` (CORE-012: synchronous).
4. Otherwise it reads the behaviour (`GetNPCBehaviorForAI`, default AggressiveMelee), runs `AIService.ExecuteNPCTurn`, and then runs the shared combat-end check `EvaluateCombatEnd` for both sides (`_Core/GameManager.CombatEnd.cs`). It also runs that check right after the turn-start Flaming Sphere tick, and returns at once when the phase is already `CombatOver`. The NPC attack paths (melee sequence, full and single ranged attack, grapple actions, charge) check after each attack whose target went out, so an AI-run killer of either side ends the fight at once (CORE-011, fixed 2026-10-08); so do an NPC cast (`TryNPCPerformSpellCastForAI`), an aura and a free-action ranged special. Once the phase is `CombatOver`, `ExecuteNPCTurn` and its routines stop at their next step (`AIService.CombatEnded`), so an AI turn neither moves nor acts after the fight has ended.

### 3.2 The turn-skip gate

`ShouldSkipTurnDueToHPState` (GameManager.cs) returns `ConditionRules.DeniesAllActions(creature)` (Combat/StatusEffects/StatusEffect.cs; batch 12 step 7, 2026-10-28, delegated RAW reading D4): the turn is skipped when `CanTakeTurnActions()` is false (HP state not Healthy, Disabled or Staggered) or the creature has a condition that denies every action: Dead, Dying, Stable, Unconscious, Asleep, Helpless, Paralyzed, Petrified, Stunned, Dazed, Cowering, Fascinated, HideousLaughter or BlownAway. `CharacterConditions.CanTakeActions()` is unchanged (it also refuses Nauseated, Panicked, Pinned and Turned); only `GrappleSystem.ShouldNPCRenewDuePin` still reads it. The PC path uses the same gate.

What now reaches the turn:

- **Nauseated**: the turn runs with `ActionEconomy.MoveActionOnly` (batch 12 step 3), a single move action (`rules/nauseated-move-only-ai`).
- **Pinned**: the grapple branch (gate 11) offers only the escape options (`BuildNPCLegalGrappleActions`), so the pinned NPC tries an opposed check or Escape Artist (`rules/pinned-escape-ai`).
- **Grappling** (batch 12 step 19): a grappling spellcaster that is the weaker grappler (its grapple modifier below its opponent's) or is pinned casts instead of grappling, through the ordinary cast candidates and the shared cast legality (`AIService.TryGrappledSpellcastForAI`, `CheckLegalityForAI` meets `GrappleSpellcastRefusal`; CMB-101; `rules/grappled-npc-casts-legal-only`); a won Move check moves the grapple to the AI decider's destination (`AIProfile.DefaultChooseGrappleMoveDestination`; CMB-103; `rules/ai-grapple-move`); after an escape the escaper's square is its decider's (`DefaultChooseGrappleEscapeSquare`), and an AI grappler always opposes an escape (D10). AI stopgaps (batch 13 catalog).
- **Panicked**: `FrightenedBehaviorController.Flee` flees (a grappling creature first tries to escape the grapple, `rules/panicked-pinned-escapes-ai`); cornered, the creature cowers without attacking and takes total defense through the catalog (`RunAIAction(total-defense)`: +4 dodge AC, no AoOs, until its next turn; no Cowering condition; batch 13 step 4, D2; `rules/total-defense-cornered-panicked-ai`).
- **Turned** (any creature type, not only undead): `AIService.ExecuteTurnedTurn` flees through the same `FrightenedBehaviorController.Flee` (grapple escape, withdraw, run or a move); cornered, the creature cowers (Cowering until its next turn; `rules/turned-undead-flees-ai`).
- **Fascinated** stays a skip; a bard's fascination whose bard is gone or dead ends in `BeginCombatantTurn`, before the gate, for both sides; a spell's fascination lasts its duration (`rules/rainbow-pattern-fascination-holds`).
- Since batch 12 step 6 (2026-10-28, decision D3) a skipped creature, PC or NPC, still gets its turn start: `GameManager.OnTurnStarted` calls `BeginCombatantTurn` (`_Core/GameManager.TurnStart.cs`) before the fork and the gate, so it regenerates, takes its Melf's Acid Arrow tick, its cooldowns pass and `StartNewTurn` resets it. Only perception (CMB-094), the Turn Undead proximity check and the compulsion pass (`BeginActingTurn`) wait for an acting turn. Combat end counts a dying or unconscious troll as out, regeneration or not (owner definition 2026-10-07, `CombatEndRules.IsOutOfFight`).

The compulsion pass (`GameManager.TryRunCompulsionTurn`, called by `BeginActingTurn`) evaluates Confused (the d% once), Suggested, Frightened or Panicked, and Turned for every acting creature and stores them on `CharacterController.CurrentTurnStart.Compulsion`; gates 4, 5, 6a, 7 and 7a read them; a fear whose source is dead or unresolved ends there and is not stored (CMB-178). For a PC it starts a forced turn instead (owner decision 2026-10-23, Q1); a cornered frightened PC gets its menu back.

### 3.4 Coroutines, pacing and action economy

- `AIService` is a component on the GameManager object. Every nested coroutine is started with `_gameManager.StartCoroutine(...)` and awaited with `yield return` (49 sites). `ResetCombatStateForNextEncounter` calls `StopAllCoroutines`, which kills AI coroutines mid-turn (CORE-013).
- `CharmedBehaviorController` now awaits its moves too (it used to start them and act mid-walk).
- **Re-check after every movement yield.** Routines test `!npc.CanTakeTurnActions()` after each move, and `MoveAlongPath` stops per step on `ThreatSystem.IsMoverIncapacitated` (dead, dying, stable or unconscious). Attacks of opportunity, Wall of Fire, Wall of Ice breaches and similar can drop a mover; one left disabled at exactly 0 hp keeps moving and acting within its single action (PHB p.145, batch 12 D5).
- Pacing: 41 `WaitForSeconds` literals in AIService (0.2-0.8 s), 17 in NPCTurns.cs (0.35-1.0 s), more in controllers and GrappleSystem. Movement is 0.08 s per square (`PlayerMoveSecondsPerStep`, CombatActions.cs:619), charge 0.06. No speed setting (AI-023).
- **Action economy is the caller's job.** Since batch 13 step 9 one move executor serves both sides (`GameManager.ExecuteMoveAction` with a `MoveRequest`): the withdraw spends its own full-round or, for a Restricted Withdraw, standard action, and an AI move passing `MoveSpend.MoveAction` is spent by the executor before its first step, as a PC's is (batch 13 step 10, decision D3, a delegated RAW reading of PHB p.138); the move first asks the shared movement legality, `MovementService.CanStartMovement` (batch 12 step 22, GRID-019): a move after a 5-ft step, a move with no move action left, a withdraw while blinded and the like are refused with a log, spend nothing and report through an optional `MoveAttempt`; each AI routine tests `npc.Actions.HasMoveAction` before moving; `UseMoveAction()` returns false and changes nothing when no move action is left (Combat/Core/ActionEconomy.cs). A move that never starts (no path, a zero move range, the mover's own square) is refused with `MoveAttempt.NoPath` and spends nothing, and each routine goes on to its next choice without its "moves toward" line; a move stopped on the way stays spent (AI-076 fixed 2026-10-29; `rules/ai-move-no-path-keeps-action-ai`, `rules/ai-move-spent-when-aoo-stops-it-ai`). Only the frightened run pays itself (its full-round action, `MoveSpend.None`), by the same reading: once the run has started, also when an attack of opportunity stops it. `EvaluateMovementOptions` still rates cells no path reaches (AI-052), so a walled-in NPC now keeps its move action but stands still. After a search move or a breath-positioning move, the melee approach is skipped; no routine double-moves (a second move would be an explicit `ConvertStandardToMove`). The breath weapon still spends no action (AI-040); the engulf and the standard-action ranged specials pay their standard action since batch 12 step 3. Use `CharacterController.CommitStandardAction()` for every standard action: a disabled creature then owes its strenuous 1 hp, dealt once the act is complete, at the turn end or by the combat-end check when the act puts a side out (`ResolvePendingStrenuousDamage`, PHB p.145), and one the damage will leave dying takes no 5-ft step, swift action or quickened spell after it (`ActionEconomy.NoFurtherActions`). The action limits themselves (single action while disabled, staggered or slowed; move only while nauseated) are set by `CharacterController.RefreshActionLimits` and read through `ActionEconomy`.

### 3.5 How a turn ends

The routine returns; unused actions are discarded (no Delay or Ready; Total Defense only for a cornered panicked creature, batch 13 step 4). `SingleNPCTurnFromInitiative` runs `EvaluateCombatEnd`, then `NextInitiativeTurn` runs True Strike expiry, pinned-duration bookkeeping, `ConditionService.OnTurnEnd`, `ProcessEndOfTurnHPState` (dying and stabilisation), the combat-end check again (no next turn once it is over), threat-preview invalidation, and advances `TurnService`. Spell effects and conditions tick at their duration anchor's initiative count (`GameManager.OnInitiativeCountReached`, before that creature's turn, PHB p.138); on wrap, `OnNewRound` ticks those with no anchor in the initiative order and the other spell trackers (SPL-032). `ConditionService.OnTurnStart` runs in `GameManager.OnTurnStarted`, before the skip gate, for PCs and NPCs alike (CMB-006).

## 4. Target selection and movement scoring

### 4.1 `SelectBestTarget` (AIService.cs:1499)

1. Takes the NPC's `LastKnownPositionTracker` (every combatant has one from `Init` since batch 12 step 8; `LastKnownPositionTracker.Ensure` adds one to a controller built another way). (The Mirror Image override that came first, the nearest image token or caster anywhere on the map, AI-036, went in batch 11 step 26 with the tokens: images are a count on the caster and every attack picks a random figure, `MirrorImageRules`, so the AI targets the caster like any creature.)
2. For each living candidate that `TeamUtility.IsEnemy` accepts, applies exclusions in order: charm source (`CharmRules.IsCharmedBy`, the predicate of the shared legality bit `AttackLegalityChecks.Charm`, batch 13 step 20, D14), fear source, Sanctuary and Hide from Undead as predictions that roll nothing (`WardSaveLedger`, batch 12 step 12, decision D6: a known failed Sanctuary save against the casting, or a mindless undead against Hide from Undead, excludes the target; an unrolled Sanctuary only scores it lower, `AttackLegality.WardTargetScoreAdjustment`, and the attack's commit rolls the one save; Hide from Undead against an intelligent undead is concealment, so the unseen-target groups below apply), and the Resilient Sphere (CMB-092).
3. Partitions into **visible** (`npc.CanSee(target, ranged)`, which only means "miss chance below 50%"; no line of sight, GRID-009) and **concealed but tracked** (has a last-known square).
4. Returns the best visible target. If none, returns the best concealed target the turn-start check pinpointed (`IsPinpointedThisRound`; the AI rolls no Listen check of its own since batch 12 step 8), else the best tracked. Unseen enemies with no last-known square do not exist for the AI (CMB-183).

`SelectBestTargetFromCandidates` (1586) tries, in order: `PriorityTargetName` exact name match (from `AITargetPriority`; 4 definitions, `grease_test_grappler1`-`4`, all naming "Slippery Sam"), then armor-priority targeting (the `AIControlFlags.ArmorPriorityTargeting` switch on `CharacterController.AIControl`, set from its control tag at spawn; before the profile since batch 13 step 7, because its only users, the `armor_targeting_test` skeleton archers, also have a profile; it reads the target's ArmorCategory fact), then the profile scorer, then the default heuristic. With a profile, the profile scorer always returns a target.

A typical melee turn calls `SelectBestTarget` twice, a kiter three times; each call re-rolls ward saves and may roll Listen.

### 4.4 Movement scoring

**`EvaluateMovementOptions(mover, targetPos, retreat, targetCharacter, profile)` (2087)**, 13 call sites.

- Candidates: every cell in one move range (`Grid.GetCellsInRange`, 3.5 1-2-1 diagonals, **current cell excluded**, so "stay" is never an option) that passes `CanPlaceCreature` and has a path from `FindPath(avoidThreats:false)`. One A* per cell (AI-019). A failed A* returns a non-null partial fallback path that the scorer accepts (AI-052).
- Parameters: `preferredRange = profile.Movement.PreferredRangeSquares` (1 without a profile; melee profiles 0; Dragon and Vampire 2; Ranged 6; casters 6; Lich 8). `avoidAoOs = Movement.AvoidAoOs && !ShouldIgnoreAoO` (false without a profile). `seekFlanking = Movement.SeekFlanking` (true without a profile).

| Mode | Score per cell |
|---|---|
| Retreat | `dist * 2` |
| Approach | `-|dist - preferredRange| * 2`; +2 if it can threaten the target from the cell; +3 if it would flank (`IsAttackerFlankingFromPosition`; only when a target character is passed) |
| Path provokes | -1000 if avoiding AoOs, else -2 per provoked AoO |

Ties keep the first cell in dictionary order. There is no cover, terrain, hazard, reach-weapon, ally-spacing or area-spread logic, and the horizon is one move action.

Other movement scorers: `EvaluateWithdrawRetreatDestination` (2143; 2× range, `dist*3 - provokes*4 - overshoot*2`); `TryTakeTacticalFiveFootStep` (1074; minimise expected AoO damage, +6 if no threats remain); dragon breath positioning (8.2); `ConfusedBehaviorController.FindBestMovementCell` (no path check); `FrightenedBehaviorController.FindBestFleeCell` (must increase distance).

### 4.5 Movement execution and attacks of opportunity

`MoveCharacterAlongComputedPathForAI` → the move entry → `GameManager.ExecuteMoveAction` (batch 13 step 9, `Combat/Actions/Executors/GameManager.MoveAction.cs`) recomputes the path with `MovementService.FindPath`, then `ExecutePathWithMovementAoOs` walks it (`MoveQuirk.AiSegmentLoop`). AoO-free stretches move in one `MoveAlongPath` call; before each step that leaves a threatened square, the shared `ResolveMovementAoOsBeforeStep` resolves that step's `ProvokedAoOs` while the mover is still in the square (PHB p.137). The same helper serves the PC's per-step walk (the same executor's), the AI withdraw (`ExecuteWithdrawMovementForAI`, first square exempt), crawl and both charge paths. Movement stops if an AoO drops the mover (0 HP or less, dead, unconscious), trips it (newly Prone, e.g. a wolf's free trip) or gives it a condition that prevents movement (`ThreatSystem.ShouldStopMovementAfterAoO`); a tripped mover still takes the other AoOs provoked at that step. `ThreatSystem.AnalyzePathForAoOs` gives each enemy at most one movement AoO per mover per round, whatever its Combat Reflexes count: `ResolveMovementAoOsBeforeStep` records each opponent listed at a step it resolves, and later paths that round (a double move, a bull rush follow after a move) skip it (PHB p.138, owner ruling 2026-10-08, CMB-128); the AI's provoke counts read the same record, though the A* threat cost still avoids every enemy's threatened squares (GRID-020). 5-foot steps never reach this code, and there is no Tumble. The executor starts with the shared movement legality (`MovementService.CanStartMovement`, batch 12 step 22), so no NPC moves after its 5-foot step (the ranged kiter's approach, retreat and search moves ask it too) or withdraws while blinded. So enemies, enemy summons and charmed, confused and frightened creatures now provoke like PCs, and the -1000/-2 path scoring and `ShouldIgnoreAoO` matter. Every caller re-checks the mover's HP after the move coroutine before acting again; charmed movement now waits for the move instead of starting it and carrying on. Not verified in Play mode. Melee routines still path toward an unseen target's true square (AI-051).

### 5.1 `ExecuteAggressiveMeleeTurn` (503)

### 5.2 `ExecuteDragonTurn` (655)

### 5.5 `ExecuteSwarmTurn` (1261)

Sticky target via `SwarmAI.ResolveTarget`. Moves directly onto the target's square (no movement scoring; only swarms share squares, GRID-005). `ApplySwarmDamageToOccupants` (1338) hits everything on the swarm's square: flat `SwarmDamage` (CRE-007) through `GameManager.DealDamage` as a natural attack (damage reduction, resistance and immunity apply, MM p.316; SPL-004), then Fort vs `DistractionDC` or Nauseated 1 round for every surviving creature on the square, without MM p.316's vulnerability and living-creature limits (CRE-060); one combat-end check after the swarm's damage; poison is log-only (CRE-014). No attack roll and no action spent. MM (Swarm subtype) checks distraction when a creature begins its turn in the swarm.

### 5.6 Healer branch (197-257)

`HealerAIProfile.DetermineActionPriority`: any ally (including itself; `TeamUtility.IsAlly` since batch 11 step 22) below 70% HP → Healing (≤25% → CriticalHealing); else castable spells and every ally ≥75% → Buffing; else castable → OffensiveSpell; else PhysicalAttack (`DetermineCombatMode`: kiter if AC < 16 or the ranged bonus is at least 2 higher than melee; melee if the melee bonus is at least 2 higher; otherwise melee only at AC ≥ 18. Melee then means DefensiveMelee or AggressiveMelee by behaviour). Heal and buff attempts call `TryExecuteSpellcastAction`; failure or OffensiveSpell → `ExecuteRangedKiterTurn`. The healer never moves to deliver a touch heal or buff and can buff itself instead (AI-047); since SPL-005 it casts no cure when nobody within the spell's range is hurt. Since batch 11 step 22 (2026-10-16) `TryExecuteSpellcastAction` walks its ranked (spell, legal target) candidates, so a cure with no ally in reach or a refused Bless passes the turn's spell to the next castable spell with a legal target (7.2); when none has one, the healer falls to `ExecuteRangedKiterTurn`, where since batch 13 step 5 it attacks a foe its weapon can reach (the attack guard, D1) and otherwise runs its out-of-reach alternatives (C1: another target in reach, the preferred range of 4 squares, `HealingRangeSquares`, then total defense), so with no foe in reach it takes total defense where it stands instead of reaching the hurt ally (AI-063, the open part; in the batch 7 soak, docs/TESTING.md 3.5, the AI-run cleric moved on 387 of its 414 turns, 298 of them Healing turns with the hurt ally out of reach; in the batch 13 midpoint soak it took total defense on 23 turns, 15 of them in seed 3). No database NPC uses this profile. In play only DMG Cleric spawns get it (`DungeonEncounterSpawner`), and those have no spells (ENC-021), so they take the PhysicalAttack branch. With spells it is reached by the scenario harness, which gives it to AI-run party clerics and druids (`Tests/Scenarios/AiProfileForClass.cs`), and by Cleric and Adept definitions built by `QuickSpawnSystem` through `NPCTemplateAIConfigurator.GetProfileArchetypeForClass` (called only by tests).

### 5.7 `GameManager.AI_SummonedCreature` (NPCTurns.cs:79)

Reached only by non-controllable summons: a summon follows its caster's control since batch 11 step 27 (`SpawnSummonedCreature`), so an NPC's summon and an AI-run party member's come here, while a player-controlled caster's summon is controllable and gets PC turns; swarm summons go to the swarm routine first. Target by `SummonCommand` (AttackNearest / ProtectCaster; ProtectCaster is effectively unreachable, CRE-032). Retreat at ≤30% HP (not a withdraw) if a move action is left. Advance if a move action is left, re-target. Then `NPCPerformAttack` (a templated summon declares its smite there, like any creature, 5.9) with a trip-only per-step evaluation for `HasTripAttack` summons: the trip replaces one step of the shared melee sequence at that step's BAB and the remaining steps attack (CMB-102; contradicts the Animal profile's "free trip on hit only"). Never casts or breathes; ignores behaviour and profile routing.

### 5.9 How attacks resolve: `GameManager.NPCPerformAttack` (NPCTurns.cs)

- Setup: break the attacker's own Sanctuary/Hide from Undead, fire pending frightful presence, `TryEnsureWeaponFallback` (since batch 13 step 15, `AiWeaponFallback.TryDraw` for every profile but the casting ones: a creature with no usable weapon in hand, no natural attacks unless mindless undead, and not empty-handed with Improved Unarmed Strike (a monk fights unarmed and keeps its flurry), draws a pack weapon through the draw entry at its RAW cost, dropping a useless bow first; it also runs at the AI turn's start, `AIService.ExecuteNPCTurnBody`), reload if needed (the catalog's reload entry, which provokes; since batch 13 step 12, AI-057 fixed, the attack follows with the standard action left, and the attack ends only when no standard action is left or the reload was refused), flank bonus, giant bombardier beetle acid spray (hard-coded by ID).
- **Smite** (CHR-020, 2026-10-14): before the melee sequence, with the target in reach, `TryNPCDeclareSmite` declares the creature's unused smite (a paladin's Smite Evil, a celestial or fiendish template smite) through `SmiteRules.TryDeclare`, the PC Smite button's core, when `AIProfile.ShouldDeclareSmite` agrees: by default only against a creature it believes is of the smitten alignment (`SmiteRules.AiBelievesSmiteFoe` reads the target's true alignment until the knowledge model exists, with no detection basis: AI-065, CHR-083). The sequence's first melee attack uses it (`CharacterController.PerformSingleAttackWithCritCore`): while a smite is declared, the step maneuver evaluation is skipped, so a summon or profile that would trip, disarm or sunder on that step attacks instead (a hit can still start a free trip). The AI also declares it before a charge's attack (`NPCExecuteCharge`), never before a bull rush on a charge.
- **One executor** (batch 12 step 17, CMB-091): melee, ranged and thrown attacks all run `AttackSequenceRequest.ForAI` through `NPCAttackSequence` -> `GameManager.RunAttackSequence`, the executor the PC Attack and Full Attack buttons use; the AI's part is `ChooseNextAttackStepForAI` (keep the target, retarget, or a melee 5-foot step) and its Haste and Improved Grab choices. The mode is the NPC's own (`AttackIntent.ChooseAiMode`, CMB-096): a ranged weapon shoots, a melee throwable is thrown only at a target beyond its reach and within thrown range (AI stopgap, batch 13 catalog), anything else is melee. Rapid Shot is declared before the first shot or throw (D9) when the creature's toggle is on or the AI chooses it, and a melee attack is two-weapon when the AI chooses it (batch 12 step 18, AI-055 closed; `AttackIntent.ForAI` asks the attack entries' `AiModeValuation` (`AIProfile.DefaultShouldUseRapidShot`, `DefaultShouldUseTwoWeaponFighting`; an interim rule replaced by batch 22's evaluator, C2, moved behind the catalog in batch 13 step 6), minimal expected-damage rules; a two-weapon request takes each step with the hand whose next attack has the higher BAB, `GameManager.TwoWeaponStepKind`). Batch 13 catalog backlog for these stopgaps: they compare expected hits (Rapid Shot, counting a thrown weapon's throws by the like weapons carried) or expected damage (two weapons) only, so they never weigh moving first against a full attack, never use a shield bash as the off hand without Improved Shield Bash (the lost shield AC is a cost they cannot weigh), and ignore Power Attack; since batch 13 step 15 the chooser never throws a creature's last weapon (`AttackIntent.KeepsAWeaponAfterThrowing`: another melee weapon in the other hand or any weapon in the pack; AI-074 fixed, C2's interim rule).
- **Melee**: the executor runs the NPC's own attack sequence one step at a time (CMB-102). The first step spends the standard action and the second turns the turn into a full attack (move action), so an NPC that moved, is slowed or can take one action makes one attack, and Haste adds one step: an iterative step to weapon and unarmed sequences, or one extra natural attack (at that attack's normal bonus) after the natural attacks of a natural sequence (PHB p.239, owner decision 2026-10-07). The AI picks that natural attack with `AIProfile.ChooseHasteNaturalAttackIndex` (default `ScoreHasteNaturalAttack` = `AI/NaturalAttackChoice.cs`: a rider that still matters against the target first (Improved Grab trigger, only against a target small enough to grab (MM p.310, CMB-126), trip, poison, disease, paralysis, petrification, energy or ability drain), then the highest attack bonus, then the higher average damage); the NPC pounce asks the same. Before each step, weapon or natural, the caller's maneuver evaluation may replace the attack (a natural attack at its own BAB: primary full, secondary -5, -2 with Multiattack; MM p.312), and the remaining steps (including the other natural attacks) continue; a grapple started that way hands the remaining steps to `AI_GrappleRestrictedTurn`. In a natural sequence the NPC makes at most one maneuver substitute (an AI limit until AI-035; PCs may replace every natural attack), and sunder needs a weapon or a natural attack at the current step that deals slashing or bludgeoning damage (`CharacterController.CanSunderWithAttack`; PHB p.158, owner ruling 2026-10-08, CMB-102), so a gore or sting step never sunders. Each attack is `CharacterController.ResolveAttackSequenceStep`, the PC iterative resolver. Profiles with `ShouldSwitchTargetsMidFullAttack` re-target between steps (optionally with a 5-ft step); other NPCs stop when their target falls or leaves reach (logged as the reason). Per hit: concentration checks, melee reactions, free trip, Improved Grab (the NPC keeps attacking after a grab). Per kill: summon cleanup; whenever the target is out of the fight (dead, dying or unconscious), the shared `EvaluateCombatEnd` check for both sides, which ends the sequence when a side is out.
- **Ranged and thrown** (since batch 12 step 17): the maneuver evaluation once, then the same step-by-step sequence as melee: the first shot spends the standard action and the second the move action (so an NPC that moved or is slowed, disabled or staggered shoots once), each shot with its weapon, reload and ammunition checks, its ward save and the AoOs it provokes from each threatening foe (`CombatFlowService.ResolveRangedAttackAoOIfProvoked`, the one helper, CMB-045); a crossbow without a free reload stops after its shot. Adaptive profiles retarget between shots.
- **Last-known misses:** after 3 consecutive auto-misses on a remembered square the target is forgotten; otherwise the NPC may spend its remaining move searching toward it.
- **Natural attacks** are used only when no manufactured weapon is equipped (`ShouldUseInnateNaturalAttackProfile`, CharacterController.cs:995). A creature with a weapon never adds secondary natural attacks (MM Introduction; CMB-077). Secondary natural attacks take -5, or -2 with Multiattack (read by the feat name, MM p.304; `CharacterStats.GetNaturalAttackSequencePenalty`).
- **Wild Shape** (CHR-020, 2026-10-14): a druid in an animal form has no weapon in hand (its gear is melded), so the same natural-attack sequence runs with the form's attacks; gate 16b sends every turn in the form to `ExecuteAggressiveMeleeTurn`.
- **Charge** (`NPCExecuteCharge`, a thin wrapper of the one charge core `GameManager.ExecuteCharge(ChargeRequest, ChargeOutcome)`, `Combat/Maneuvers/ChargeCore.cs`, batch 12 step 21): decision by `ShouldNPCCharge` (the charge action: a full-round action, or a partial charge for a creature limited to one action, D2, `ChargeRules.HasChargeAction`; melee weapon; not in reach; `CanChargeTarget`, which reads the core's `CanCharge`) and `profile.ShouldPreferCharge`. The core is the PC's: path AoOs, flanking from the end square, the AI's smite declaration, Spirited Charge for a mounted charger, the +2 (PHB p.154) as a per-attack term on the single attack or an untyped `CharacterStats.Bonuses` entry on each pounce step, the pounce's natural attacks and rakes as steps of the attack sequence (`AttackSequenceRequest.ForCharge`), the victory check.
- **Maneuvers:** `ShouldUseManeuver` (2241) → coup de grace first (profile or `UseCoupDeGrace` override, full-round, helpless adjacent target); else `profile.GetPreferredManeuver` re-validated (Trip needs a standing target and any melee or natural weapon; Disarm, Grapple, Sunder their checks); without a profile the legacy chooser `TryNPCSpecialAttackIfBeneficial` (AI-025). Every maneuver runs the one maneuver core (`ManeuverRequest.ForAI`, `GameManager.ExecuteManeuver`, batch 12 step 19: the item a disarm or sunder aims at is `AIProfile.ChooseManeuverItem`, D15; a feint before the attack is `AIProfile.ShouldFeint`, a stopgap). Trip, disarm, sunder and grapple cost one step of the NPC's attack sequence at that step's bonus (`ManeuverActionCost.For`, `CharacterController.TryCommitManeuverSubstituteStep`; the first step spends the standard action), bull rush a standard action, coup de grace the full round (an overrun is not an attack step: `AIProfile.ShouldOverrun` plans a move through the defender's space before the move, `GameManager.TryPlanOverrunPathForAI`, through the one overrun, batch 12 step 20); the evaluation runs before every step of the NPC melee attack, weapon or natural. On its own it would chain maneuvers (Humanoid: trip, disarm an armed target, grapple; Berserk: trip, grapple; Grappler: trip, disarm, grapple; null profile: trip, disarm at STR mod 3+, grapple at STR mod 4+) and retry failed ones. A stopgap in `AIService.TryExecutePreferredManeuver` (owner decision 2026-10-07, AI-060, to be replaced by weighted personality scoring) keeps a per-creature, per-turn memory (`CharacterController.AIManeuverMemory`, `AI/AIManeuverTurnMemory.cs`, cleared in `StartNewTurn`): once a maneuver succeeds the AI makes no further maneuver that turn and attacks with the remaining steps, so a trip that lands is followed by attacks on the prone target; after a maneuver fails (a lost check or an attempt foiled by an AoO) it does not retry that type against that target that turn, and the step attacks unless the evaluation picks a different maneuver (a profile picks one maneuver per step, so in practice it attacks; the legacy chooser's pick is read through `GameManager.PeekNPCFallbackManeuverForAI`). A grapple that takes hold still hands the remaining steps to `AI_GrappleRestrictedTurn`, and coup de grace, the profile order and the legacy chooser are unchanged. The executor reports what it attempted and whether it succeeded (`TryNPCSpecialAttackByTypeForAI(..., out succeeded)`, `TryNPCSpecialAttackIfBeneficialForAI(..., out attempted, out succeeded)`). The ranged kiter's pre-attack maneuver goes through the same check; the summon trip in `AI_SummonedCreature` does not (it is trip-only and stops once the target is prone). Inside a natural-attack sequence any step can be a maneuver, rolled at the BAB of the natural attack it replaces, and the other natural attacks follow (AI-035, CMB-102; owner decision 2026-10-07). Grapple, sunder, trip and disarm provoke from the target (not with the matching Improved feat), bull rush from every threatening enemy (Improved Bull Rush spares only the defender; 25% of the others' AoOs strike the defender) and coup de grace from every threatening enemy, all through `ResolveManeuverInitiationAoOs`, shared with the PC wrapper; after a successful bull rush the NPC's stay-or-follow choice comes from `AIService.ChooseBullRushPush` -> `AIProfile.ChooseBullRushPush` (default: follow for the maximum; CMB-098); a foiled attempt still spends the action (disruption rule: CMB-083; a disarm fails only on AoO damage). The opposed-check math follows PHB p.154-158 in the shared `CharacterController.Resolve*` methods (CMB-014 fixed; not verified in Play mode).

### 5.10 The attack guard and the out-of-reach alternatives (batch 13 step 5)

Owner ruling (2026-10-29): AI creatures "should never waste an attack on something they can't hit"; what they do instead comes from their behaviour profile. Decision D1 and design choice C1 of `docs/designs/action_catalog_batch13.md`, recorded in `docs/systems/RULES_COVERAGE.md` 4.1.

- **The guard** (`AI/AiAttackGuard.cs`, pure): `Check(attacker, target[, intent])` with the AI's own intent (`AttackIntent.ForAI`). In order: the shared direct-attack legality with reach and range (`AttackLegality.CanDirectlyAttack`: the reach of the melee weapon in hand and a reach weapon's dead zone, PHB p.112, p.137; five increments thrown and ten projectile, PHB p.134; the sphere, the hold bar, a known failed ward), Bar Reach or Range; ammunition for a projectile weapon (PHB p.113), Bar Ammunition; a crossbow loaded or reloadable with a free action or the move action left, Bar Ammunition. A ward save still to roll is allowed with its risk; concealment, spell range and line of effect do not count. NPCs and AI-run party members alike; an omniscient read of the target's square until batch 17.
- **Callers:** every routine's attack target (`AIService.SelectGuardPassingTarget`, built on `SelectBestTarget`'s new filter): the aggressive melee, defensive melee, kiter and dragon routines, the kiter's blind attack, the concentration turn; the attack step choice (`ChooseNextAttackStepForAI`, `SelectAdaptiveFullAttackTarget`'s default); the summoned creature's attack; the charmed and confused controllers; and a backstop in `GameManager.NPCPerformAttack` before anything is spent (no ward break, no frightful presence): "makes no attack: target is out of reach", or for an empty quiver `CheckAmmoAvailable`'s refusal line.
- **Alternatives** (`AIProfile.GetOutOfReachAlternatives`, `AiOutOfReach.Run`, through `GameManager.RunAIAction`; an AI choice): melee and mixed profiles attack another guard-passing target, then approach to the weapon's reach (`ApproachRangeSquares`); ranged profiles another target, then the preferred range, then an approach; a casting profile (`IsCastingProfile`) that is not Melee style and holds no usable ranged weapon (`HasUsableRangedWeapon`) another target, the preferred range, then total defense. After a move the standard action left attacks (AI-045). An approach is skipped when the weapon cannot shoot at all, the move to the preferred range when the creature has no ranged means at all (`AIService.HasRangedMeans`: a ranged weapon that can shoot, a throwable weapon, or a castable spell beyond touch), and an unloaded crossbow is reloaded first when a bolt is there to load (`GameManager.TryReloadEquippedWeaponForAI`, the catalog's reload entry through `RunAIAction` since batch 13 step 12, which provokes, D4; `ReloadRules.Check` refuses without one, PHB p.113-114, the PC's Reload button too; with the standard action left after it the list goes on). The melee routines (aggressive, defensive, dragon), the summon's attack, the concentration turn's "can attack now" and the compulsion controllers ask the guard for melee reach only (`AiAttackGuard.IntentFor(..., allowThrow: false)`, C1): a melee creature with a dagger or spear closes in and never throws its only weapon; the kiter allows throws, and its approach with a throwable weapon stops at thrown range (`ApproachRangeSquares(npc, allowThrow)`). The attack step chooser asks the guard and the sequence's own stage-3 reach test (`StepTargetLegality`), one predicate with the executor. An archer with an empty quiver therefore declares no shot; since step 15 it drops the bow and draws its stowed weapon instead (AI-073 fixed).
- Scenarios: `rules/ai-attack-only-in-reach-npc` and `-party`, `rules/ai-kiter-approach-then-attack`, `rules/ai-kiter-no-ranged-no-retreat`, `rules/ai-kiter-no-ranged-no-retreat-ranged-profile`, `rules/ai-reach-weapon-dead-zone`, `rules/ai-archer-no-ammo-no-shot`, `rules/ai-melee-thrower-no-throw`; static tests in `AttackLegalityTests` (`TestAiGuard*`) and `AIProfileFrameworkTests` (`TestOutOfReachAlternativeDefaults`).

### 6.5 Mapping gaps and surprising combinations (inferred)

- **Spellcaster-profile monsters hover.** `SpellcasterAIProfile` leaves `CombatStyle` Melee, so aboleth, mind_flayer, ogre_mage and the rest run AggressiveMelee with a preferred range of 6: the approach scoring parks them about 6 squares away with nothing in reach. They reach melee only by charging. None has spells anyway (AI-038).
- **Range-2 tie.** Vampire and Dragon profiles prefer range 2. For a reach-1 creature an adjacent cell scores -2+2 and a 2-square cell 0, so grid order decides whether it closes (AI-038). The vampire (`SeekFlanking` true) does close when an adjacent cell would flank (+3); the dragon never gets that term.
- **Ranged monsters' weapons.** Since ITM-004 was fixed (2026-10-08) a creature the AI runs through the ranged routine (`AIService.RoutesToRangedTurn`: RangedKiter, or a profile whose `CombatStyle` is Ranged, not DefensiveMelee) spawns holding the bow its data lists under `EquipSlot.Ranged` (bralani, elf_warrior, erinyes, medusa, drider) and carries its melee weapon. Since batch 13 step 15 (D8) it draws that weapon through the draw entry when the bow cannot shoot (no ammunition; `AiWeaponFallback`), but an archer with arrows adjacent to a foe still fires and provokes: choosing to switch is batch 22's evaluator. A melee creature carries its listed bow and never uses it. A kiter whose bow id is missing (the halfling_warrior's light crossbow, CRE-052) still spawns holding its longsword and oscillates (AI-039). The lantern archon's ray and manticore spikes are not modelled as ranged attacks.
- **Trip loop.** Humanoid, Berserk, Grappler and all null-profile NPCs trip every standing target in reach with their first attack step (136 effective definitions, AI-035, CMB-102). Since the stopgap of 2026-10-07 (AI-060) a trip that lands is followed by attacks on the prone target, and a failed trip is not retried against that target that turn; before it, the later steps went to the next maneuver (disarm, then grapple) or retried the trip. No odds or size check (PHB ch.8 limits trips by size).
- **Breath only through the Dragon profile.** ankheg, behir, chimera, digester and gorgon never breathe (AI-033).
- **Mephits** use Animal (PackHunter) tactics; their breath and SLAs are text (CRE-017).
- **Class maps disagree.** `DungeonEncounterSpawner.UpdateAIForClass` vs the test-only `NPCTemplateAIConfigurator`: Sorcerer Evoker vs Spellcaster, Adept Spellcaster vs Healer, Paladin Humanoid vs Ranged, Barbarian Berserk vs Humanoid. ### 6.6 Target facts and the knowledge provider (batch 13 step 7)

Target scoring reads typed facts about a target, never display tags (`Assets/Scripts/AI/Knowledge/`, namespace `DND35.AI.Knowledge`):

- **Facts.** `FactKey`: Species, CreatureType, Subtype, Size, ArmorCategory, ShieldCarried, WieldsMelee, WieldsRanged, WieldsReach, WoundBand, HpState, Condition, Caster, Mindless. Each `FactValue` carries a `KnowledgeLevel` (Unknown, Apparent, Inferred, Recalled, Confirmed). `WoundBand` (`KnowledgeFacts.WoundBandOf`): Unhurt at full hit points, Hurt at half or more, BadlyHurt below half and above 0, Down at 0 or less.
- **Views.** `KnowledgeService.ViewOf(observer, subject)` asks `KnowledgeService.Provider` (an `IKnowledgeProvider`) for an `ITargetView`. Until batch 17 the provider is `OmniscientKnowledgeProvider`: every fact is the true value at Confirmed, read live (species is the shown race, `DisplayedRace`; creature-type spellings are normalized at read time through `CreatureTypeProgressionDatabase.TryParseCreatureType`, so "MagicalBeast" equals "Magical Beast" and a wild-shaped creature's current type is read; armor from the worn armor's category; hands from the held items). **Batch 17 swaps the provider** for one that knows only what the observer has perceived, identified or recalled (`docs/designs/enemy_ai_knowledge_and_personalities.md`); **batch 14 scores the facts** in weighted personalities. Nothing outside the provider reads the truth for target scoring; one exception in spell scoring: `AISpellcastingStrategist.GetEnemyTypeScore` penalizes a mind-affecting spell through the shared immunity rule (`SpellUtilities.IsImmuneToMindAffecting`, the one the resolver applies, plants included per MM p.313) rather than a fact, until batch 17 exposes the immunity as one.
- **Queries.** `FactQuery.Parse` turns each `TagPriority` string into a typed query on one fact (cached); every shipped string converts ("Race: X", "Armor: ... Armor", "Unarmored", "Wielding: Unarmed", "HP State: X"); the parser also takes "Type:", "Subtype:", "Size:", "Wound:" and "Condition:"; an unknown prefix parses to nothing and scores nothing. A bare word that names no fact parses as a species (`FactQuery.IsSpeciesFallback`); no shipped string uses that fallback and every shipped species names a `RaceDatabase` race (`KnowledgeFactsTests`), so a misspelled weight fails the suite. "Wielding: Unarmed" (and bare "Unarmed") is `QueryKind.NoManufacturedWeapon`: no manufactured weapon in either hand, the old display tag's meaning, not unarmed in the PHB p.139 sense (a monk, a creature with Improved Unarmed Strike or natural weapons, and a caster holding a touch charge are armed yet match it); batch 14 must not read it as RAW armed or unarmed. The armor fact reads the armor's typed category (`ItemData.ArmorCat`; base category, so mithral looks like its base armor), not display tags. A query matches whole: "Elf" is not "Half-Elf" (AI-078). "HP State: Staggered" reads the BadlyHurt band (PHB p.313: staggered is nonlethal damage equal to current hit points); Disabled and Dying stay hit point states.
- **Readers.** `AIProfile.AddFactPriorities` (base `ScoreTarget` and the three undead profiles), `AIService.GetTargetPriority`'s creature-type term, `AIService.GetArmorPriorityScore`, and `AISpellcastingStrategist.IsCreatureType`/`IsMindless`. The strategist's undead, construct and ooze checks read the CreatureType fact only; a mindless creature of another type no longer counts as undead, and a mind-affecting spell against it gets its own -50 (MM p.313, mindless creatures are immune to mind-affecting effects).
- **Control flags.** `AIControlFlags` (MagicMissileOnly, ArmorPriorityTargeting, ScenarioOverride) on `CharacterController.AIControl` are set from the control tags at spawn (`AIControlFlagRules.FromTags`; the scenario overrides through `GameManager.AddAIControlTag`) and cleared on slot reuse; `AIService.IsMagicMissileOnlyCaster` and `UsesArmorPriorityTargeting` read them, not the tags. The unread `Class:` display tag was dropped.

### 7.2 `TryExecuteSpellcastAction` (AIService.cs)

Since batch 11 step 22 (2026-10-16) the turn's spell is an ordered walk over ranked candidates (AI-063, single-target part):

1. Needs a standard action and `IsSpellcaster`. `ScoreCastableSpells` (7.3) gives the castable spells with their scores, best first (equal scores keep the spell list's order, so the first is the spell the old single pick took); none: return false.
2. `RankedSpellCandidates` builds the (spell, target) candidates in that order as the walk asks for them. `AISpellcastingStrategist.EvaluateDefensiveCasting`: 2 = no adjacent non-ally; 1 = adjacent but estimated Concentration (DC 15 + level) ≥ 50%, or HP ≤ 25% with a Healing/Escape spell; 0 otherwise. 0 → the spell is skipped (this replaced the old `SelectLowerLevelAlternative`: the next-ranked spell that is safe to cast is taken). 1 → log, then cast. The roll happens at cast time (7.6): `ResolveNPCSpellcastProvocation` uses the real threat list (reach included, so a caster threatened only by a reach weapon also gets the choice) and `AISpellcastingStrategist.ShouldCastDefensively`, the same ≥ 50% or desperate-heal test. Then `SelectBestSpellTarget` (7.4), which returns only a target the cast core would accept; a spell with none is skipped.
3. `AiSpellCandidates.TryInOrder` tries the candidates with `TryNPCPerformSpellCastForAI` (an area candidate with `TryNPCPerformAreaSpellCastForAI`, 7.8); a false return (the cast core refused the cast and spent nothing, for example the decider's refusal of a weapon buff with no weapon to enchant) passes to the next candidate, at most `AiSpellCandidates.MaxTries` (5) tries. No candidate cast → the routine falls back to non-spell actions.

There is no spell-versus-weapon comparison and no score floor (except the dragon turn's filter and floor, 5.2, AI-037): the best castable spell with a legal target is cast. Since batch 11 step 25 (2026-10-16) area spells are candidates too, each with the aim its search found (7.8), within the same cap of 5 tries; summon and escape spells are no more castable than before.

### 7.3 `SelectSpell` and scoring

`SelectSpell` (the AI's planning: the spell it would cast, also asked to size an approach before the caster is in range, so it tests no target or range) returns the first of `ScoreCastableSpells`. Candidates: `GetCastablePreparedSpells` (distinct prepared unused IDs; known spells with a slot for spontaneous casters; 0-level spells only while an unspent 0-level slot or spontaneous 0-level spell remains, since 2026-10-14, SPL-138; `rules/zero-level-spells-ai`: an AI-run neutral_mage_test with four prepared Dazes casts exactly four in six rounds, verified in Play mode; since batch 11 step 17 it also leaves out a spell whose components the caster lacks, the cast core's own rule `SpellComponentRules.Refusal`: the pouch, the divine focus, a priced focus or material component; every NPC caster spawns with its kit, `SpellComponentKit`, so only a lost pouch or symbol or a missing priced component filters a spell). `TryNPCPerformSpellCast` then asks `CanCast`, which gives the same answer for a spontaneous caster since 2026-10-14 (SPL-123). Since batch 11 step 22 the caster's own legality tests are the cast core's (`GameManager.CheckCasterLegalityForAI`: the metamagic, the casting action, Silence and the components, in the Legality stage's order), so a spell any of them refuses is skipped. Buff/Illusion spells already active on the caster are skipped. Score = `SpellcasterAIProfile.ScoreSpell` for that family, else `AISpellcastingStrategist.ScoreSpellComprehensive`. Highest wins, even if negative.

`SpellcasterAIProfile.ScoreSpell` (AI/Profiles/SpellcasterAIProfile.cs:49): school priority ×10 (unlisted school = 10); AoE terms on the best aim the caster has (`EvaluateAOECast` over `AreaAimSearch`, 7.8, since batch 11 step 25: +3 per enemy hit, -10 per ally casualty, -6 below minimum, -1000 unsafe or no aim; for a beneficial area such as Bless +3 per ally helped, -1000 with no aim); single-target preference ±4-5; buff already active -1000; buff-first +4 above 65% HP; conserve 3rd+ level -1/-4; counter +2; utility -5; plus the strategist. ### 7.4 Spell targeting (`AISpellcastingStrategist.SelectBestSpellTarget`, 226)

Since batch 11 step 22 (2026-10-16) every candidate must pass the cast core's own legality test for the NPC cast (`GameManager.CheckLegalityForAI`: `CheckLegality` on `SpellCastRequest.ForAI`, the NPC caster tests, `IsValidTargetForSpell` with its creature-type and HD limits and its sight test for an enemy target, the range to the target's footprint at the cast's caster level, the metamagic, the casting action, Silence and the components), so the AI picks only casts the core lets through; without a GameManager (a static test) the old range test stands in. Allies and enemies are told apart by `TeamUtility.IsAlly` and `IsEnemy` (a Neutral creature is neither).

- Self spells: the caster, when legal.
- Ally spells (Healing, non-Area Buff, SingleAlly): `SelectBestAllyTarget` scores allies in range (healing by missing HP, frontliner bonus; buff skipped if already active); returns no target for a healing spell when no ally in range is hurt (an unhurt ally, an undead and a construct score negative infinity, SPL-005), and the **caster** for a buff when no ally is in range and the caster is a legal target. Magic Weapon, Greater Magic Weapon and Keen Edge take only a creature that wields a weapon the spell could enchant (`AiCastChoiceHeuristics.ChooseWeapon`, 7.6) and never fall back to the caster. Touch range means adjacent only, so a healer often buffs itself (AI-047).
- Enemy spells: `ScoreEnemyTarget` (wounded, casters, save exploit, resistance, kill bonus, SR); the Mirror Image priority target that used to win went in batch 11 step 26 (AI-036). The SR chance is `(21 + CL - SR)/20`, clamped, with Spell Penetration in the caster level (PHB p.177: 1d20 + caster level at least the SR; `AISpellcastingStrategist.SpellResistanceSuccessChance`; AI-046 fixed in batch 11 step 28, it was 20 points too low); below 50% costs 15, below 75% costs 5. No immunity filtering beyond the legality test; that test refuses a target behind a Resilient Sphere or one whose Sanctuary or Hide from Undead save the caster already failed (the cast core's `CheckCastDirectAttackLegality`, batch 12 step 12), and a ward not yet saved against scores 10 lower (`AttackLegality.WardTargetScoreAdjustment`). Every single-enemy spell also needs a target the caster can see (PHB p.175; `CanSeeSpellTarget`, with or without a GameManager, because `IsValidTargetForSpell` skips its sight test for Daze, the charms, Dominate Person, Hold Monster and some others, SPL-185). Falls back to the passed target only when it is an enemy and the cast at it is legal (the step 22 review: the Healer branch passes a wounded ally, AI-047, and the walk goes on to offensive spells, which must not land on that ally).

Since batch 11 step 25 (2026-10-16; the area half of AI-001) an NPC casts area spells through the same cast core as a PC's area cast. Which spells: every spell the PC's targeting routes to the area cast (`AreaAimSearch.IsAreaCastForAI` reads the PC routing's own predicate, `AoESystem.RoutesToArea`: an Area target type, PHB p.175, or an area shape, so also Prayer, a burst centred on the caster with a Self target, PHB p.264) and Grease, cast on its 10-ft. square (PHB p.237). A single-target request of any of them is refused for every caster ("area-needs-aim"), so each has one cast path (the step 25 review moved Prayer off its own single-target resolver, which no request reaches now).

1. **Aim search** (`AI/Spellcasting/AreaAimSearch.cs`), on the cast's own clone (a Widened area is searched widened). Candidate placements, at most 200 (`MaxCandidates`), in a fixed order: a burst or cube at every grid intersection from which its area reaches a square the spell is aimed at (the enemies' for a harmful spell, the caster's side's for a beneficial one): for a burst every intersection whose count to the square's far corner is within the radius, for a cube every intersection whose cube holds the square, so the origins on an enemy's far side that spare an ally beside it are searched (step 25 review); ordered by the count to the nearest aimed square's far corner (each square's own corners first), then nearest the caster's space, kept when the origin is within the spell's range and in the caster's line of effect (`AoESystem.IsOriginWithinRange`, `HasLineOfEffectToOrigin`); a self-centred burst from each of the caster's creature-centred origins; a one-square effect (Flaming Sphere, Call Lightning) on each such square in range; a cone from each intersection on the edge of the caster's space in each of the 8 directions; a line from each of those intersections through the centre of each such square (a line along a grid line crosses no square's interior); Grease's square on each 2-by-2 block that holds such a square, within range. Each footprint keeps only the squares its origin reaches (the Legality stage's filter). The creatures it takes in are those the spell's filter takes (`AoESystem.IncludedByFilter`, `TeamUtility`), any square of their space counting (`IsCreatureInCells`).
2. **Score.** A harmful spell (Damage, Debuff, Control): +1 per enemy it would affect (standing, not inside a globe that stops it, not immune to a mind-affecting spell or to its damage, within its own limits: Sleep 4 HD or fewer, Halt Undead undead, Calm Animals animals; `AreaAimSearch.WouldAffect`), less 2 per ally it would affect (`DefaultAllyWeight`; the caster counts). A beneficial spell (a buff whose filter takes only the caster's side, such as Bless): +1 per ally not already under it, -1 per enemy it would help. Prayer (PHB p.264): +1 per ally and per enemy it reaches that is not already under it. Any other area (light, obscurement, terrain, walls, figments, summons) has no judged value and no placement (AI-071). An aim is worth casting when it affects an enemy (a helped ally for a beneficial spell) and scores above 0 (Prayer: above 0); the best is the highest score, then fewer allies harmed, then the first found. A profile adds its ally sparing (`SpellcasterAIProfile.FindAreaPlacement`: the allies' expected casualties within `AOECasting`), and its `EvaluateAOECast` reports the best aim for the spell's score (7.3). No worthwhile, safe placement: the spell is no candidate (no fallback).
3. **Legality and cast.** `AIService.ScoreCastableSpells` scores an area spell on the clone its cast uses (a prepared metamagic slot's metamagic applied, the clone its aim was searched on) and keeps it only with a placement the cast core would accept (`GameManager.CheckAreaLegalityForAI`: the decider's capability, the origin's range and line of effect, the NPC caster tests, the metamagic, the casting action, Silence, the components); the walk (7.2) casts it with `GameManager.TryNPCPerformAreaSpellCastForAI`, which builds `SpellCastRequest.ForAIArea` (or `ForAIGrease`) with the placement's squares and the creatures in them (`AoESystem.GetTargetsInArea`) and runs it with `RunCastSync`; the combat-end check follows a cast that counts.
4. **AI follow-ups** (AI-071). The AI decider reports what it cannot choose (`AiCastDecider.CanFill`), and the cast core refuses such a request at Legality ("choices-incomplete", nothing spent): the ends of Wall of Fire, Wall of Ice and Wall of Force; Grease on a held object or worn armor (or with no form chosen); the square a Summon Swarm places its swarm on. Not a rule fork: the PC's decider fills the same choices through its dialogs. The AI also never moves to a better square before an area spell (its planning, `SelectSpell`, runs the full area legality from the current square, so a caster whose only useful spell is a cone or a short-range burst never approaches to cast it), never directs its Flaming Sphere, and does not value obscurement or terrain areas. The aim search treats a spell's `AoEFilter` as the rules: a spell whose data spares the caster's side where the PHB area takes every creature (Acid Fog, Flame Strike and others, SPL-195) is aimed as if it spared allies.

Scenarios: `rules/npc-cast-fireball` (an Enemy-team wizard's Fireball takes the three heroes and spares its kobold ally), `rules/npc-cast-bless` (allies only), `rules/npc-cast-web`, `rules/ai-area-aim` (a Sound Burst at an origin that takes four clustered heroes and not the kobold beside them, then a Burning Hands cone at the cluster), `rules/prayer-cast` and `rules/prayer-globe` (a Scripted NPC Prayer through the area cast since the step 25 review); static suite `Tests.AI.AreaAimSearchTests`.

### 7.9 Summons, Dimension Door and held touch charges (batch 11 step 27)

Since batch 11 step 27 (2026-10-16; SPL-091 and SPL-115 closed) an NPC summons, teleports and holds a touch charge through the same cast core as a PC.

1. **Summons** (PHB p.285-288). `AIService.ScoreCastableSpells` keeps a Summon Monster or Summon Nature's Ally only when `GameManager.CheckSummonLegalityForAI` passes: the cast core's legality on the summon request (`SpellCastRequest.ForAISummon`, the NPC summon path's quirks `LegacyCastQuirkSets.AiSummonPath`) and the AI decider's choices on a copy. The walk casts it with `GameManager.TryNPCPerformSummonSpellCastForAI`; at the Legality stage the AI decider (`AiCastDecider.FillChoices` -> `GameManager.AiFillCasterChoices`, `AiFillSummonChoices`) takes the list of the spell's own level (one creature, PHB p.285), the creature with the highest challenge rating among those that fight (`AiCastChoiceHeuristics.ChooseSummonOption`; then the most Hit Dice, then the list's order) and the free square nearest the caster's nearest foe within the spell's range and the caster's line of effect (`FindAiSummonSquare`, through `FindBestAdditionalSummonCell`; the cast core then tests the square for every caster, `CheckSummonPlacementLegality`); the shared Summon resolve rolls the count (`RollSummonCreatureCount`), spawns the creatures on the caster's side, inserts each into the initiative right after the caster and registers it, as for a PC. A summon follows its caster's control (`SpawnSummonedCreature`), so an NPC's creatures take the `AI_SummonedCreature` turn (5.7). A single-target request of a summon spell is refused for every caster ("summon-needs-placement"). Summon Swarm stays out: the AI cannot place a swarm (AI-071). Scoring is unchanged (`GetSummonScore`: caster alone +20, outnumbered +15, +2 per spell level, base +6).
2. **Dimension Door** (PHB p.221). Its destination is a cast choice, `CastChoices.Destination`, which both deciders fill with the escape square (`GameManager.ChooseDimensionDoorDestination`: a free square within range, at least 2 squares away, farthest from the nearest foe, preferring one beside no foe; a PC square picker is UI-053); the handler reads the choice and no longer forks on `IsPlayerControlled`. The AI casts it only in danger (`AISpellcastingStrategist.GetEscapeScore` above 0: hit points at 25% or less, or 40% or less beside a foe, or 50% or less with three foes adjacent) and with a free escape square.
3. **Held touch charges** (PHB p.141, p.176). The AI's touch delivery (`AiCastDecider.TouchDeliveryFor`, its `ChooseTouchDelivery`): cast now when the target is in reach; else hold the charge only when a move this turn reaches a free square beside the target (`GameManager.FindTouchApproachSquareForAI`); else not at all (approaching over several turns is AI-047); a spell whose charge cannot be held (Chill Touch, `SpellData.CanHoldCharge`, PHB p.176) is never held. A hostile melee touch spell with no foe in reach but one a move reaches, and that the spell is worth landing on (`AISpellcastingStrategist.IsWorthwhileSpellTarget`: not an undead an inflict spell would heal, not a creature a globe shields), becomes a HoldCharge candidate (`SelectHoldChargeTarget`); the walk casts the hold (`GameManager.TryNPCHoldTouchChargeForAI`, `SpellCastRequest.ForAIHoldCharge`, the PC's HoldCharge resolve), and the turn's end moves the caster beside the foe and delivers (`TryDeliverHeldChargeTurn`, through `GameManager.TryNPCDeliverHeldChargeForAI`: the PC Discharge button's request with the AI decider, `SpellCastRequest.ForAIHeldChargeDelivery`). A charge held from an earlier turn is delivered at the turn's start (gate 16c), to the creature in reach with the fewest hit points, or after a move to the nearest one a move reaches, among the creatures the spell is worth landing on (the same `IsWorthwhileSpellTarget`, so a held cure never goes to an undead ally); the delivery's cost is the PC's (`HeldChargeDeliveryRules`, batch 12 step 18), so no AI-only limit is needed. For every caster, a melee touch that misses in its casting round holds the charge and casting another spell dissipates it (the cast core; RULES_COVERAGE 4.1). Since batch 12 step 18 the delivery costs an attack (a foe, after the casting round) or a standard action (a friend), on both sides (SPL-156 closed).

Scenarios: `rules/npc-summon-monster`, `rules/npc-dimension-door`, `rules/npc-held-touch`, `rules/npc-hold-charge-approach`, `rules/cast-pair-mirror-image` (Mirror Image needed nothing here: batch 11 step 26 made it one RAW model); static checks in `Tests.Magic.CastCoreStageOrderTests` (the builders and the summon refusal) and `Tests.Magic.SummonMonsterAlignmentRulesTests` (the AI's creature choice).

### 7.10 Metamagic choice (`AiMetamagicPlanner`, batch 11 step 28)

Since batch 11 step 28 (2026-10-16; AI-001 closed) the AI weighs metamagic as one more dimension of its spell candidates (`AI/Spellcasting/AiMetamagicPlanner.cs`). Tactics only: every candidate passes the same cast-core tests as a plain one (`CheckCasterLegalityForAI`, the target or aim legality), so the rules stay `MetamagicSystem`'s for both sides.

1. **Candidates.** A prepared caster's metamagic was chosen when it prepared: its prepared metamagic slots are listed as their own spells (`GetCastablePreparedSpells`, batch 11 step 21). A spontaneous caster (sorcerer, bard, a monster that casts as a sorcerer) gets, after its plain spells, one candidate per known spell and known metamagic feat (`SpellcastingComponent.GetKnownMetamagicFeats`; Heighten at most twice: to the highest level it has a spell per day of, and, with a globe on the field, to the lowest such level that gets through every globe) that `MetamagicSystem.ValidateMetamagicApplication` allows (never Quicken on a spontaneous cast, PHB p.98; a feat that can modify the spell; never Silent Spell on a bard spell, PHB p.100; at most 9th level) and `CanCastWithMetamagic` can pay for (a spell per day of the adjusted level, PHB p.88), and that could matter now (`CouldMatter`: Empower and Maximize on a spell with dice; Extend on a buff, debuff or control effect that ends within 10 rounds; Heighten on a save spell against foes or with a globe on the field; Enlarge when a creature it is for stands only within the doubled range; Widen on an area; Silent inside a Silence; Still while grappling or with arcane spell failure). One feat per candidate, at most 24 per decision (`MaxSpontaneousCandidates`), the known spells walked from the highest level down so the cap keeps the strongest spells' options. Each is a clone of the known spell carrying the choice in `SpellData.AiChosenMetamagic` (and `MetamagicDataRef`), which `SpellCastRequest.AiMetamagicFor` reads, so its legality probes, target choice (the range prefilters and the approach sizing read `AiMetamagicPlanner.RangeSquaresFor`, the range of an Enlarged candidate's cast clone; only a custom range doubles today, SPL-010, so a close, medium or long spell gets no Enlarged candidate), area search (on the widened or enlarged clone; the search cache key counts the heightened level, which the globe test reads), hold and cast all carry the metamagic; a cast that takes the full round (a spontaneous metamagic one, PHB p.88) is never held to move and touch, since no move is left (`AIService.SelectHoldChargeTarget`, PHB p.141); the template is never touched and `SpellData.Clone` drops the choice. A spontaneous caster with no plain spell left at a level can still cast a known lower spell with metamagic.
2. **Value** (`AiMetamagicPlanner.Value`, added to the candidate's score in `AIService.ScoreCastableSpells`; it replaced `AISpellcastingStrategist.GetMetamagicScore`): Empower and Maximize add 0.35 per point of expected damage or healing gained (`SpellDiceRules.ExpectedTotal` on the spell's dice at the cast's caster level, the custom resolvers' dice through `CustomSpellDice.TryDamageEstimate`, Magic Missile per missile; healing capped at the recipient's missing hit points), times the creatures the area takes (foes less allies harmed), plus 15 when only the boosted average drops the target; Extend 0.5 per round gained within a 10-round horizon per creature; Heighten 1.5 per save DC point per foe, plus 20 when it gets through a globe that stops the plain spell on the target (PHB p.95, p.236); Quicken 8 (the action it leaves). A spontaneous choice then pays 2 per spell level it adds (the higher spell per day) and 3 for the full-round action of a spell that takes less (PHB p.88: the move it gives up). Silent, Still, Enlarge and Widen add nothing of their own: they win when the plain spell cannot be cast or reach, or when the wider area's own aim scores more. Example: a sorcerer 10's Empowered Fireball outranks the plain one when the burst takes two or more foes and not against one.
3. **Cost.** The candidates are scored up front (an area search is cached per board and footprint, `AreaAimSearch.BoardKey`, which counts the area radius and range since this step); the walk still tries at most `AiSpellCandidates.MaxTries` casts. The `[AI][Spell]` log names the metamagic ("Empowered Fireball") and a metamagic candidate's score.

The damage estimates of the strategist and of the profile casters (`AISpellcastingStrategist.EstimateAverageDamage`, `SpellcasterAIProfile.EstimateAverageDamage`) read the custom resolvers' dice too since this step (Fireball and Lightning Bolt were estimated at 1d6). Scenarios: `rules/ai-metamagic-choice` (three foes: Empowered Fireball, 90 each, a 5th-level spell per day, the full round), `rules/ai-metamagic-choice-one-foe` (plain Fireball, the move left) and `rules/ai-metamagic-hold-plain` (a sorcerer holds a plain Shocking Grasp to move and touch rather than a full-round Empowered one); static checks in `Tests.AI.AiMetamagicPlannerTests`. Not covered: two feats on one cast and a cleric's spontaneous conversion with metamagic (AI-072), metamagic rods (ITM-018).

### 8.5 Regeneration

`ApplyRegenerationAtTurnStart` heals a fixed amount unless suppressed by the right damage tag or HP ≤ -10. It runs at every creature's turn start, before the skip gate (`BeginCombatantTurn`, batch 12 step 6, D3), so a paralyzed or dying troll regenerates too. MM regeneration (damage becomes nonlethal) is not modelled; troll and others behave as fast healing (CRE-017). No profile reasons about regeneration (own or enemy).

### 8.6 Grapple AI

- **Starting a grapple:** via `GetPreferredManeuver`/`ShouldInitiateGrapple` (Aggressive always; InitiateWhenSafe if STR mod ≥ target's; never with Improved Grab), or the legacy chooser (STR mod ≥4). Improved Grab is a free attempt on a qualifying hit, automatic for NPCs.
- **In a grapple** (`AI_GrappleRestrictedTurn`, up to 8 iterations, 20% random early stop): a raker that began its turn grappling rakes first (any profile; the rake makes the turn a full attack, MM p.314, CMB-144); predatory animals then attack with a natural weapon on every grapple action (one natural attack each, the highest bonus unless another has a rider that matters, `ChooseGrappleNaturalAttackForAI`; one grapple action per step of the BAB ladder, so a lion makes one, PHB p.156, MM p.314, CMB-127, CMB-146), escape below 25% HP and deal grapple damage while pinning; an AI pinner whose pin is due (its next turn after pinning; PHB p.156, a pin lasts 1 round, CMB-120) pins again first only when that pays (`ShouldNPCRenewDuePin`: it keeps a grapple attack for damage this turn, or another able ally is in the fight and the target is a spellcaster or that ally is adjacent to it); otherwise it takes the pinning choices below, which let the pin lapse, so a lone one-attack pinner keeps dealing damage instead of renewing every round while the pinned target can only try to escape (D4); a predator below 25% HP that pins releases the pin (free, ends the grapple) before any escape check; others, when pinning, Damage > Use Opponent's Weapon > Disarm 50% > Move 20% > Release 5%; otherwise Pin 35% (STR mod ≥3), light weapon 45%, opponent's weapon 30%, unarmed 20%, then Damage. Releasing a pin is a free action that ends the grapple for both creatures and moves neither (PHB p.157, CMB-089 fixed; `CharacterController.IsFreeGrappleAction`). The AI rolls the release only before it has spent an attack this turn, so no iterative attacks are lost, and `AIService.RunGrappleTurnForAI` / `CanContinueTurnAfterGrappleEnded` then let `ExecuteNPCTurn` carry on with a normal turn (standing up first if prone) while the standard action is unspent (not verified in Play mode). Several chosen sub-actions are stubs (CMB-033). Non-animal NPCs escape only when pinned (since batch 12 step 7 a pinned NPC gets its turn, `rules/pinned-escape-ai`). `GrappleBehavior.EscapeOnly`/`Avoid` are ignored once grappled (AI-029). After an Improved Grab trigger hit the AI chooses between grappling normally and holding with the body part that hit (`AIProfile.ShouldHoldWithImprovedGrab`, default `DefaultShouldHoldWithImprovedGrab`: hold only while another able foe is in reach and the -20 check keeps even odds; MM p.310, CMB-148); a holder lets go at the start of its turn when no other foe is in reach (before its Damage check, so it deals no hold damage then, AI-064), and otherwise opens its turn with the hold's Damage check (at -20, in place of its first attack) when that check keeps even odds (`AI_ImprovedGrabHoldDamageCheck`, CMB-163); the AI always deals hold damage lethal (the default; nonlethal at -4 is the PC's choice, delegated RAW reading 2026-10-14); it starts no second grab while it holds. A grab that takes hold during the attack sequence turns the remaining steps into grapple actions, and a confused or charmed grappler's attack becomes its grapple turn (PHB p.156, CMB-149).

### 8.8 Perception and wards

- `CanSee` = miss chance below 50% (`GetMissChance`: invisibility unless See Invisibility or Glitterdust, Darkness, Wall of Fire, Entropic Shield, incorporeal, Displacement). No line of sight (GRID-009); no blindsight, tremorsense or blindsense.
- One last-known store since batch 13 step 7 (AI-021): the `LastKnownPositionTracker` every combatant has from `Init` (`CharacterController._lastKnownTargetPositions` was removed). Its writers record only real observations: a sighting (`RecordSighting`, only when the owner can see the creature: target scoring and an attack at a seen target), a Listen pinpoint, and the square a creature vanished from, for the enemies that saw it go invisible. It is cleared when a controller is reused for a new creature (CRE-046) and, for continuing party members, in `ResetCombatStateForNextEncounter`.
- `AttemptListenChecks`: See Invisibility users roll opposed Spot vs Hide; everyone else rolls one Listen vs a flat DC 20 that pinpoints all concealed targets (one check per turn, at turn start). A pinpoint records the square and lapses when the creature leaves it; a deafened creature fails automatically (PHB p.307). DMG p.295 (Invisibility) opposes the Listen check with Move Silently (DC 0 in combat), adds distance and obstacles, and pinpoints only when the check beats the DC by 20 (CMB-182). The turn-start call is shared with PCs (`PerceptionRules.ProcessTurnStart`, CMB-094).
- Sanctuary and Hide from Undead: see 4.1. The attacker's Sanctuary save is rolled once per (attacker, casting) when the attack is attempted, for PCs and NPCs alike, and a failure loses that attack (`WardSaveLedger`, batch 12 step 12, D6). An intelligent undead's Hide from Undead save is rolled at its turn-start perception step; a failure leaves the warded creature unseen by it (total concealment) and loses no attack, and a success no longer ends the spell; a mindless undead never perceives the creature. `BreakProtectiveWardsOnAttack` runs on the PC attack path and `NPCPerformAttack` only, not on charges, maneuvers, grapples or spells (SPL-094, partly unverified).

### 8.9 Morale and fleeing

There is no morale system, surrender or rout. The only retreats are: DefensiveMelee withdraw below 30% HP, summon retreat at 30%, the animal grapple escape below 25%, the dragon's post-breath step, the kiter's distance keeping, and condition-forced fleeing (Confused 51-70, Frightened, Panicked and, since batch 12 step 7, Turned). `FleeHealthThreshold`, `GaseousFormFleeThreshold` and `Aggression` are unread (AI-010, AI-026). The 3.5 core books leave monster morale to the DM; the DMG offers guidance rather than a mechanic. What blocks a morale system (no map exit, victory and XP only at HP ≤ 0) is in 11.8.5.

## 11. Extension points

### 11.1 Add a tactical routine

1. Add the value to `NPCAIBehavior` (NPCDatabase.cs:808) and fix its XML doc (AI-031).
2. Route it in `ExecuteNPCTurn`: the profile branch (267-278), the no-profile switch (283-297), and the Healer PhysicalAttack sub-branch if relevant. Dragon, swarm and summon routing bypass behaviour.
3. Write `private IEnumerator ExecuteXTurn(CharacterController npc, CharacterController target)` following AggressiveMelee: HP check on entry; charge via `SelectBestAction`; `EvaluateMovementOptions` → `MoveCharacterAlongComputedPathForAI(npc, cell, GetPlayerMoveSecondsPerStepForAI())`; **HP check after every movement yield**; **test `HasMoveAction` before moving** and call `UseMoveAction` (it returns false when no move is left); re-target; maneuver or `NPCPerformAttackForAI` (it chooses full vs single attack); fallback `TryAIWallInteraction`. Call `TryExecuteSpellcastAction` if it should cast. Start nested coroutines with `_gameManager.StartCoroutine` and `yield return` them.
4. Set `AIBehavior` in data and in the other writers (2.4). The value reaches the AI through `_npcAIBehaviors`; keep it aligned (AI-015).

### 11.2 Add a profile

1. `AI/Profiles/<Name>AIProfile.cs`, `namespace DND35.AI.Profiles`, `: AIProfile` or `: SpellcasterAIProfile`.
2. Set tuning in `public override void ApplyRuntimeDefaults()`: call `base.ApplyRuntimeDefaults()` first, then set `ProfileName` (unconditionally, for readable logs) and the rest. Never write tuning in `OnEnable`, which keeps only runtime-state resets, so saved `.asset` values survive (AI-017). Set `CombatStyle` explicitly; `SpellcasterAIProfile` leaves it Melee, so a creature with it runs a melee routine, which casts first when it has a combat spell (batch 13 step 8) and otherwise holds at its preferred range (AI-038). Create instances with `GameManager.CreateRuntimeAIProfile<T>()` (tracked and destroyed by the GameManager) or, outside the spawn path, `AIProfile.CreateRuntime<T>()`; a bare `ScriptableObject.CreateInstance` gives the base defaults.
3. Override hooks from 6.3. Keep score magnitudes in mind: visibility terms are ±50-120.
4. Add an `NPCAIProfileArchetype` value and a `case` in `GameManager.RuntimeAIProfileTypeFor` (`return typeof(T);`; `BuildRuntimeAIProfile` creates and tracks it); set the archetype on definitions. `AIProfileFrameworkTests` fails when a value but None maps to no profile.
5. Needs its own routine (like Healer or Dragon)? Add an `is` branch in `ExecuteNPCTurn` before line 266.
6. Per-turn state on the instance is safe only because every creature gets its own instance; never share one.
7. Unit-test it in `AIProfileFrameworkTests` (`AIProfile.CreateRuntime<T>()`, never a bare `ScriptableObject.CreateInstance<T>()`, which has no tuning; then `TestHelpers.Cleanup` or `DestroyImmediate`).

### 11.3 Add a `*ForAI` wrapper

AIService may use only public GameManager members (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md conventions; AI-024). Add an expression-bodied delegate next to the block at GameManager.cs:10884-11041, e.g. `public bool TryNPCPerformSpellCastForAI(...) => TryNPCPerformSpellCast(...);`. Coroutine executors return the `IEnumerator`; AIService runs them with `_gameManager.StartCoroutine`. Existing wrappers: 44 in GameManager.cs (re-counted 2026-10-29); the uncalled turn-undead and undead-test wrappers were deleted in batch 13 step 2 (AI-053), and `HasActiveShieldSpellForAI` stays for its scenario caller.

### 11.4 Add an NPC action (attack form, monster ability, special action)

1. **Executor:** a GameManager partial next to `NPCPerformAttack`, `NPCExecuteBreathWeaponForAI`, `NPCExecuteCharge`, or in AIService if it needs no GameManager privates (`TryExecuteEngulf`); a trigger every bearer fires goes in `Combat/Special/GameManager.MonsterTriggers.cs` (`TriggerFrightfulPresence`, `ProcessTurnStartAura`). Expose GameManager executors with a wrapper.
2. **Action economy:** spend the action yourself (`CommitStandardAction`, `UseFullRoundAction`, `UseMoveAction`), or run a catalog entry that does (the breath weapon commits its standard action since batch 13 step 18).
3. **Rules plumbing:** resolve AoOs where PHB ch.8 says the action provokes (movement: `ResolveMovementAoOsBeforeStep`; maneuvers: `ResolveManeuverInitiationAoOs`; casting: `ResolveNPCSpellcastProvocation`); route damage through `GameManager.DealDamage` with a typed `DamagePacket`; saves through `SaveRollRules.Roll` (or `SavingThrowResolver`/`SpellSaveResolver`, built on it) with a `SaveContext`; a touch attack through `SpellCaster.SpellTouchAttack` and `TouchAttackAC`; end with `EvaluateCombatEnd` (both sides; CORE-011) when the action can drop a creature.
4. **Call site:** free actions at the top of `ExecuteNPCTurn` (after the condition gates); standard actions before or after movement in the routines that should use it. Prefer a capability check usable by every routine over adding it to AggressiveMelee only.
5. **Data:** a new ability kind needs an `NPCDefinition` field, a deep copy in `NPCDefinition.Clone`, a `CharacterController.ConfigureX`, a call in `InitializeNPCFromDefinition`, a clear in `CharacterController.ResetForNewCreature` (CRE-046), and the template copy lists (DEVELOPMENT_RECIPES "Add a monster or NPC"). Tick its cooldown at the turn start (`CharacterController.TickTurnCooldowns`, called from `GameManager.BeginCombatantTurn` for every creature) and roll its recharge with `CharacterController.RollRechargeRounds`.
6. **Feedback:** `CombatUI?.ShowCombatLog(CombatLogHelper...)`, a `[AI][Tag]` `Debug.Log`, and a `WaitForSeconds`.

Maneuvers go through `ManeuverPreferences` → `GetPreferredManeuver` → `ShouldUseManeuver` → `TryNPCSpecialAttackByTypeForAI`; see DEVELOPMENT_RECIPES "Add a combat maneuver".

### 11.5 Add a condition-forced turn

Write `Combat/Behaviors/<Name>BehaviorController.cs` with `TryBuildDecision` and `ExecuteDecision`; add a lazy field and `TryGet...TurnDecisionForAI` / `Execute...TurnDecisionForAI` wrappers in GameManager; call them in `ExecuteNPCTurn` before targeting (73-111). A condition in `ConditionRules.DeniesAllActions` skips the turn before any controller runs.

### 11.6 Add a spell-scoring term

Add a static `Get<X>Score` to `AISpellcastingStrategist` and sum it in `ScoreSpellComprehensive` (1115); constants at 31-87. Profile-specific terms go in a `ScoreSpell` override. ### 11.7 Pitfalls

- `_npcAIBehaviors` is index-parallel to `NPCs`; summons append and remove (AI-015, CRE-006).
- Every movement yield can kill the mover; re-check HP.
- `StopAllCoroutines` on encounter reset kills AI mid-turn (CORE-013); turn advancement is re-entrant (CORE-012).
- `SelectBestTarget` has side effects (ward saves, Listen, tracker writes); do not call it speculatively in a loop without accepting them.
- Spawn-time `aiProfile` assignments are overwritten by `BuildRuntimeAIProfile`; change the archetype instead.
- Duplicate creature IDs: the later registration wins (CRE-022).
- Every PC action runs through a catalog executor since batch 13 step 16; call the entry through `RunAIAction` or `RunAIActionNow`, never the PC button handler (they open menus or continue the PC turn). Turn undead (`ActionIds.TurnUndead`, `TryNPCTurnUndeadTurnForAI`), rage (`ActionIds.Rage`, `TryNPCRageForAI`) and the domain powers (`ActionIds.DomainPower` with `Variant` the domain) since step 16. Aid another and waking an ally are catalog executors since batch 13 step 13: run them through `RunAIAction` (`ActionIds.AidAnother` with `Target` the ally, `Opponent` the foe and `Variant` offense or defense; `ActionIds.WakeAlly`).

Noise that buries AI lines: per-call A* logs (GRID-011), threat rebuild logs (CMB-039), `[AOO-DEBUG]`.

### 12.4 Cost and pacing

- **CPU (estimated, not profiled):** each `EvaluateMovementOptions` call runs one A* (with allocations and a log line) plus a path-AoO analysis per candidate cell; candidates grow from 60 cells at move 4 to 432 at move 12 (capped by the 20×20 grid). Withdraw searches double range; dragon breath positioning adds a breath-area computation per cell per enemy. `GetAllCharactersForAI` allocates a new list on every call (21 call sites in AIService, 38 project-wide). All of it runs synchronously between yields, so it shows as a frame hitch (AI-019, CMB-039).
- **Wall clock (summed from literals):** turn start 0.6 s; stationary full attack about 1.6 s; move plus single attack about 2.1 s plus 0.08 s per square; kiter shot about 2.4 s. A round with eight goblins is roughly 15-20 s (AI-023).

## 13. Toward deeper AI (analysis for the owner to decide)

This section is analysis, not a plan. It describes structural constraints and options; the owner decides direction.

### 13.1 Structural constraints

- **Decisions are encoded as control flow.** The routine order (cast, then breath, then move, then attack) *is* the decision. Adding an option means adding a branch to each routine that should consider it, which is why engulf, specials and breath each live in one routine only. `SelectBestAction` is the last vestige of a scoring layer that was never finished (AI-027; the uncalled `EvaluateAttackOptions` and `EvaluateBestManeuver` were deleted in batch 13 step 2); its `Retreat` result is worth keeping for morale (13.3).
- **A bare `ScriptableObject.CreateInstance` of a profile subclass has no tuning** since batch 13 step 2 (AI-017): it carries the base `AIProfile` defaults (`ProfileName` "Default AI", Melee). Create profiles with `GameManager.CreateRuntimeAIProfile<T>()` on the spawn path, else `AIProfile.CreateRuntime<T>()`. A profile `.asset` made from the Create menu also starts from the base defaults (there are none today).
- **Scores are not comparable.** Target scores, movement scores, spell scores and maneuver preferences use unrelated scales (and visibility swamps targeting). There is no common unit such as expected damage, expected HP removed, or expected conditions imposed, so "spell versus full attack versus breath" cannot be compared.
- **The rules engine is asymmetric** (full audit and unification plan: [PC_NPC_PARITY.md](PC_NPC_PARITY.md)). The breath weapon spends its standard action since batch 13 step 18 (AI-040 fixed; saves and damage shared since batch 12 step 10 and SPL-004); monster triggers and auras still run on AI turns only (CRE-039); AoOs on movement, maneuvers and casting, and casting Concentration, now follow the PC rules. Any smarter AI built on top would optimise against rules the player does not share. These asymmetries are also the main source of "the AI feels wrong" reports that are not AI bugs.
- **Capabilities are data the AI cannot see.** Spell-like abilities, stench, constrict, secondary breath and many MM specials exist only as text or unread fields; most monsters that should cast cannot. A deeper chooser has little to choose from until the capability data is executable.
- **One-move horizon, no memory, no team.** Movement looks one move ahead, profiles are stateless except for dragons and swarms (each creature's only other memory is the per-turn maneuver stopgap, AI-060), and NPCs share nothing.
- **Performance budget.** Per-cell A* already causes hitches; any search over action sequences needs a shared reachability flood and a cached threat map per turn first (AI-019).
- **Testability and the AI-depth metric.** The Play-mode scenario harness ([TESTING.md](../TESTING.md) 3.4-3.5) runs `ExecuteNPCTurn` in the real scene: whole fights with seeded and forced dice, a typed trace of every turn, attack, AoO, maneuver, NPC cast, move and condition, and rules invariants; actors can be AI-run, scripted (typed steps through the same `*ForAI` executors, after the compulsion gates), idle or PC-driven. Its 62 `rules/*` scenarios pin shared mechanics. The AI-vs-AI soak `soak/dmg-random-encounters` (the AI-run Quick Start party against DMG dungeon random encounters, seeds 1-50, run with `ScenarioHarness.QueueFresh("soak/*", "1-50", "perSession=10")`) is the AI-depth baseline: its `soak` statistics give per-team actions per turn (baseline 2026-10-07: party 0.17 attacks, 0.15 maneuvers and 0.28 casts per turn; enemies 0.50 attacks, 0.21 maneuvers and no casts; 13 of 50 fights reached the 30-round cap; the full regression soak of 2026-10-08 after the session's maneuver and grapple changes: enemies 0.40 attacks per turn, 12 fights at the cap, table in TESTING.md 3.5). Compare a change to the AI against the latest table and read the traces of the seeds that moved. In both baseline tables seeds 25-30 and 37-40 fought a creature that kept an earlier allip's Babble aura and incorporeality (CRE-046, fixed 2026-10-08); since the fix a seed's trace no longer depends on the session split, and TESTING.md 3.5 has the rechecked results of seeds 21-40, so compare those seeds with the recheck, not with the tables. The soak surfaced AI-047 (the Healer cleric cures itself at full HP on 468 of 624 turns), AI-059, CMB-121, CMB-123, SPL-121, CRE-044 and CRE-046 (fixed); earlier smoke runs surfaced AI-058 and confirmed CORE-011 (fixed 2026-10-08). AI-018 (the unguarded `CombatUI` uses in `ExecuteNPCTurn`) still blocks a headless run outside the scene.

### 13.2 Prerequisites (fix before tuning behaviour)

Ordered by how much they distort what the AI experiences:

1. Rules parity on NPC paths: the monster triggers and auras run for every bearer since batch 13 step 19 (CRE-039 fixed; the allip's babble and the cloaker's moan still use the generic aura, CRE-079; the breath weapon's standard action is shared since batch 13 step 18, its saves and touch rolls since batch 12 step 10, damage since SPL-004, recharge since batch 12 step 6).
2. Capability data that works: spells for the lich, vampire, DMG class levels and Spellcaster monsters (SPL-015, CRE-030, ENC-021), or an SLA system (CRE-015, CRE-017); ranged weapons equipped (ITM-004, done 2026-10-08) and a draw or swap action for NPCs; null profiles mapped (AI-004, done 2026-10-29); `Ranged` behaviour handled (AI-003, done 2026-10-29).
3. Plumbing: behaviour stored on the NPC instead of the parallel list (AI-015); one target filter `IsTargetableBy(attacker, target, action)` with cached ward saves used by every selector (the ward and sphere part is `AttackLegality` since batch 12 step 12; AI-049, AI-050); a decision trace log.

### 13.3 Options for a deeper decision layer

- **Unified candidate-action evaluation.** Generate legal options each turn (move, double move, 5-ft step, full attack, single attack, charge, each maneuver, each castable spell or SLA with its best target or area placement, breath, item use, withdraw, total defense, delay/ready once they exist) and score each in one unit, for example expected HP removed from enemies plus a value for conditions imposed, minus expected damage taken (including AoOs provoked) and resources spent. Profiles would become weight vectors and veto rules over this scorer instead of routines. The existing routines could remain as fallbacks while options migrate.
- **Area spells and positioning.** Done for casting by batch 11 step 25 (2026-10-16, 7.8): the AI aims an area with `AreaAimSearch` and casts it through the PC's area resolve. Left: moving to a better casting position before an area spell (the search scores only the caster's current square), sharing the search with breath positioning, and the AI follow-ups of AI-071 (walls, Grease on an object, a summoned swarm's square, obscurement and terrain areas, directing a Flaming Sphere).
- **Two-step planning.** Score "move now, full attack next round" and "5-ft step plus full attack", and reach-weapon spacing; needs the reachability cache.
- **Group tactics.** A per-side blackboard: claimed targets for focus fire, flank pairing (complementary squares), Aid Another and waking sleeping allies (11.8.1), protecting casters and healers, spreading against known area casters.
- **Morale.** A data-driven flee/surrender policy per profile (read `FleeHealthThreshold`, `Aggression`), leader-loss checks, intelligent undead never fleeing. It would consume the `Retreat` result that `SelectBestAction` already produces, with the HP test replaced by the policy and called from every routine, not only DefensiveMelee. That is also the intended fix for AI-027: act on the `Retreat` result instead of reducing `SelectBestAction` to a charge test as the issue's suggested fix proposes. It first needs a way off the map and a defeated state that victory and XP honour (11.8.5).
- **Perception per RAW.** Opposed Listen/Move Silently, Spot/Hide, senses (scent, blindsense, tremorsense) and line of sight (GRID-009), so AI information matches what the creature could know (fixes AI-051).
- **Intelligence tiers.** MM Intelligence scores could select how much of the option space a creature considers (mindless: nearest target, no maneuvers; animal: instinctive charges and grabs; intelligent: full evaluation and team play), which also gives the unused difficulty setting (AI-012) a natural meaning.
- **Encounter-level behaviour.** DMG ch.3 random encounters bring surprise and encounter distance; the AI currently has no notion of surprise, ambush or retreat off the map (CMB-028), which matters for the encounter-driven game loop.

Each option depends on the prerequisites above; the cheapest high-value sequence is probably rules parity first, then the decision trace and target filter, then the unified evaluator for melee options, then spells and areas, then team play.
